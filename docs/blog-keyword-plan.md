# GymLedger Blog Keyword Plan

Content plan for the getgymledger.com blog. GymLedger is an iOS workout tracker: quick set logging, an auto-start rest timer, PR and volume charts, custom splits, a bodyweight log, kg/lbs switching, and no ads or trackers. Researched October 2026.

**About the data:** we had no keyword tool, so this plan has **no search-volume numbers**. Difficulty is a qualitative estimate based on who ranks today (app-company blogs, big publishers, supplement brands):
- **Low:** few pages target the exact query, or the results are weak forums, scrapers or thin pages.
- **Medium:** app or fitness blogs cover it, but there is room for a better long-form page.
- **High:** big publishers (BarBend, Men's Health, Stronger by Science) and several app blogs compete for it.

Before writing, check volumes in a keyword tool (Ahrefs, Semrush, Google Keyword Planner or Search Console) and re-rank the priorities.

**Priority key:**
- **P1:** strong app fit, clear intent, and realistic difficulty. Write first.
- **P2:** good fit, but more competition or a weaker tie to the app.
- **P3:** brand and topical-authority fill-in. Write later.

**Feature claims:** only claim features that GymLedger is known to have. Ideas marked *(verify)* depend on a feature this brief can't confirm (supersets, notes/RIR fields, data import). Check the app before claiming them.

## 1. Competitor findings

### StrongLifts (stronglifts.com): the site GymLedger is modeled on
- **No blog or archive.** The homepage is a conversion page: a spreadsheet email signup, an app download, then program cards (5×5, Plus, Ultra, Intermediate, Madcow, Quarantine, Lite, Mini).
- **Two content types do the SEO work:**
  1. **"Definitive" exercise guides** for the squat, bench, deadlift, row, overhead press, pull-ups and dips. The squat guide is roughly 11,000 to 14,000 words (a 46-minute read) with about 25 diagrams. Its H2 skeleton is: Why form matters, Basic technique (stance, depth, muscles worked), Safety (knees, failing reps), Detailed technique (about 20 H3s, from feet to breathing), Common issues (7 H3 fixes), Stretches, Equipment, Variations, FAQ.
  2. **Program hub pages.** The 5×5 hub is short (about 300 words of prose) and links out to sub-guides: Quick Start, How to Progress, Assistance Work, Failure, Plateaus, Top/Back-Off Sets.
- **Monetization pattern:** a free spreadsheet lead magnet with email capture, a Pro discount, affiliate equipment links (Amazon), and app badges with social proof ("100k+ five-star reviews", "30m+ workouts logged").
- **Lesson for GymLedger:**
  - Build a few very deep evergreen pillar guides plus program hubs, and link them to each other.
  - Put an app call to action in every guide, framed as "the app does the logging and progression math for you".
  - A free printable log or spreadsheet is a proven lead magnet.

### Hevy (hevyapp.com/blog): the largest SEO footprint among tracker apps
- About 12 blog pages of roughly 10 posts each, plus separate Guides, an Exercise Library, a Workout Splits hub, and standalone SEO landing pages such as `/rest-between-sets/`.
- **Topics covered:**
  - Every split length: 2-, 5-, 6- and 7-day splits, PPL (3/4/5/6-day), upper/lower, bro split and 5×5, including 5×5 plateau and warm-up posts.
  - List-style exercise posts ("8 Isolation and 7 Compound Chest Exercises…") for every muscle group.
  - Dumbbell, kettlebell and cable workouts.
  - Science: sets per muscle, RPE, rest between sets, strength vs hypertrophy, junk volume, volume vs intensity, drop sets.
  - Tracking: "The Best Way to Track Progressive Overload", "How to Track Workouts".
  - Beginners: first day at the gym, beginner mistakes, beginner gains, strength training over 40.
  - Pop culture: One Punch Man workout.
- **Gaps:**
  - Nothing India-, kg- or Hindi-specific.
  - Little on the mechanics of logging (dumbbells, bodyweight lifts, warm-ups).
  - Nothing on sticking to a split (missed days, switching programs).
  - Little linking bodyweight trends to training.

### Boostcamp (boostcamp.app/blogs): about 26 pages of posts
- Split comparisons ("Upper Lower vs PPL", "Is the Bro Split Effective"), exercise alternatives (leg press, bench press), lift comparisons (RDL vs conventional deadlift, flat/incline/decline bench), and 1RM testing.
- Powerlifting vs weightlifting, powerlifting diet and bulking length.
- **App roundups:** "Best Free Workout Apps for Beginners (2026)", "Workout App vs Personal Trainer", and a `/best/workout-apps` page.
- Anime and character workouts (Toji Fushiguro) and creatine rules.
- **Takeaway:** app companies openly publish "best apps" roundups that include themselves.

### Fitbod (fitbod.me/blog)
- Categories: Training, Nutrition, Cardio, Product.
- Uses its own data ("…data from 148 million exercise sets").
- Covers cutting and bulking, HIIT plus lifting, "More Reps or More Weight?", short full-body workouts and TRX.
- Heavy on nutrition (bulking foods, metabolism).
- Product posts explain its algorithm.

### JEFIT (jefit.com/wp)
- Categories: Guide, Workout Tips, Exercise Tips, Nutrition Tips, General Fitness, What's New.
- Recent focus: periodization and mesocycles, progressive overload (two separate guides), training volume, cluster sets, free weights vs machines, carb cycling, and "muscle longevity".
- Most posts tie to a product feature (Adaptive Plan, Load Progression).

### Strong (strong.app)
- **No blog.** The site is marketing pages, a Pro page, a testimonials wall, a swag store, a help center and press mentions.
- Strong owns no SEO content, so "Strong app alternatives" searches are answered by other vendors.

### Liftosaur (liftosaur.com/blog)
- A developer and product blog: docs, the planner, its Liftoscript language, PWA and infrastructure posts, plus one "How to start weightlifting" post.
- Not an SEO competitor.

### Setgraph, Lyfta and other newer apps
- They publish large volumes of comparison and how-to content, often in several languages (`/es/`), for example:
  - "Hevy vs Strong App (2026)"
  - "Timed Rest vs Resting by Feel"
  - "Workout Record Guide"
  - "How to Track Progressive Overload"
- Comparison and "best app" results are already crowded with vendor pages. GymLedger should only enter those with a distinct angle (privacy, lifetime purchase, India and kg, auto rest timer).

### India results (checked "gym diet plan India vegetarian" and "gym workout plan for beginners in India")
- **Diet results** are dominated by supplement and e-commerce brands (MuscleBlaze, HealthKart, InstaCuppa, Mettle) and paid PDF sellers.
- **Beginner workout-plan results** have no strong India-specific editorial page. Generic Western pages rank, and the JEFIT "india workout plan" routine is a 6-day plan labeled beginner.
- **Opportunity:** honest, training-first, kg-native, tracking-oriented India content, with a Hindi version later (the app already ships in `hi`).

### Content gaps GymLedger can own
1. **Logging mechanics:** how to log dumbbells, bodyweight and weighted bodyweight exercises, warm-ups, and microloading in kg. Almost nobody covers this, and it is exactly what a tracker app solves.
2. **Sticking to a split:** missed days, switching splits, and how long to run a program.
3. **Bodyweight log plus strength:** weigh-in frequency, why the scale goes up after starting the gym, and relative strength.
4. **India, kg-first and Hindi:** training-first content instead of supplement sales pages.
5. **Privacy-first, no-subscription tracker:** angles for comparison posts (Hevy-without-social, lifetime purchase, no ads).
6. **Realistic progress expectations:** monthly strength gains and PR types, tied to monthly-gains charts and dated PR milestones.

### Recommended article format (adapted from StrongLifts)
- **Length:** 2,000 to 4,000 words for spoke articles; 6,000+ for pillars (progressive overload, splits, the big lifts).
- **Structure:**
  - An answer-first intro (a featured-snippet-ready 40 to 60 words).
  - A table of contents.
  - H2 sections: why, how, examples, common mistakes, variations, FAQ.
  - FAQ schema.
- **Visuals:** at least one worked example table (a sample 4-week log in kg *and* lbs), plus diagrams or screenshots.
- **App tie-in:** an in-context "How to do this in GymLedger" box with app screenshots, plus one closing call to action. No hard sell above the fold.
- **Lead magnet:** a free printable or PDF training log in kg/lbs, mirroring the StrongLifts spreadsheet strategy.
