The best 3 day split workout for most people is **full body three times a week**, on Monday, Wednesday and Friday or any three days with a rest day between them. It trains every muscle three times a week and gives each one 48 hours or more to recover. If you'd rather have more focused sessions, **upper/lower/full** hits everything twice a week. **Push/pull/legs** run once a week is the weakest of the four for most lifters, and **FBEOD** (full body every other day) is the full-body idea on a rolling cycle that works out to three or four sessions a week.

Below you'll find each option with complete workouts (sets × reps), weekly sets per muscle, Mon/Wed/Fri versus rolling schedules, who should pick which, and how to add weight in kg and lb. If push/pull/legs is what you're really after, our [push/pull/legs routine guide](/blog/push-pull-legs-routine) covers the 6-day version. This post is about getting the most out of three days.

## The 3 day split workout options at a glance

Every 3 day gym split is a trade between how often each muscle gets trained and how long each session runs. These are the four that people run in practice.

| Split | Typical week | Each muscle trained | Session length | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full body x3 | Mon A, Wed B, Fri C | 3x a week | 50-70 min | Beginners, and most people |
| Upper/lower/full | Mon upper, Wed lower, Fri full | 2x a week | 50-65 min | Lifters who like focused days |
| Push/pull/legs | Mon push, Wed pull, Fri legs | 1x a week | 65-80 min | Experienced lifters who enjoy it, or a stopgap |
| FBEOD | A and B, every other day | 3-4x a week | 45-60 min | Shift workers, irregular weeks |

Session lengths assume 2-3 minutes of rest on the big lifts and about 90 seconds on the small ones. If you rest five minutes and check your phone between sets, add 20 minutes to everything.

## How much training frequency really matters

Lifters argue about frequency far more than the research justifies. Three meta-analyses cover most of it.

- A 2016 meta-analysis of 10 studies by Schoenfeld, Ogborn and Krieger found that, with weekly volume matched, training a muscle twice a week beat once a week for growth. Whether three times beats twice was left open ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27102172/)).
- A larger 2019 follow-up from Schoenfeld, Grgic and Krieger pooled 25 studies and found no significant difference between frequencies when weekly volume was equal. Where volume wasn't equal, higher frequency had a modest edge ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30558493/)).
- A 2024 meta-analysis of 14 studies (392 people) comparing split routines with full-body routines found similar strength and muscle gains when volume was matched ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38595233/)).

So weekly hard sets are the main driver, and frequency is mostly the way you deliver them. That matters a lot on three days. Twelve chest sets in one session means the last six are done tired. Spread across three sessions, every set gets a fresh effort. That's the real problem with a 3-day PPL. Once a week can work, but squeezing 10 or more good sets per muscle into one session makes it long, and the back half is rarely your best work.

For how many weekly sets to aim for, see our guide to [how many sets per muscle group per week](/blog/how-many-sets-per-muscle-group-per-week). The short version is roughly 8-12 hard sets per muscle for beginners and 10-16 for intermediates.

### Weekly sets per muscle in each plan

These are the direct hard sets per week in the four plans below. Compound lifts also train helpers (presses hit triceps, rows hit biceps), so arms get more work than the direct numbers suggest.

| Muscle | Full body x3 | Upper/lower/full | PPL x1 | FBEOD (average) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chest | 8 | 6 | 9 | 5-6 |
| Back | 9 | 9 | 10 | 10-11 |
| Shoulders | 7 | 7 | 10 | 8-9 |
| Quads | 8 | 9 | 9 | 8-9 |
| Hamstrings | 7 | 8 | 6 | 8-9 |
| Biceps (direct) | 2 | 2 | 5 | 3-4 |
| Triceps (direct) | 2 | 4 | 5 | 3-4 |
| Calves | 3 | 3 | 4 | Optional |

The totals are similar, and most land between 6 and 10 sets, which suits a beginner. What changes is how they're spread across the week and how long each session runs. Intermediates can add a set to the first three lifts of each session to push the big muscles toward 10-12.

## Option 1: Full body three days a week

This is the classic 3 day full body workout split, and it's the one we'd pick for anyone in their first year. You squat, press and pull in some form three times a week, which builds technique fast, and no session is a marathon.

Use three different days (A, B, C) so you aren't benching heavy three times a week. Each day has one lower-body compound, one press, one pull, then a few smaller lifts.

**Day A**

| Exercise | Sets × reps |
|---|---|
| Back squat | 3 × 5-8 |
| Bench press | 3 × 6-8 |
| Barbell or chest-supported row | 3 × 8-10 |
| Lying leg curl | 2 × 10-12 |
| Lateral raise | 2 × 12-15 |
| Triceps pushdown | 2 × 10-12 |

**Day B**

| Exercise | Sets × reps |
|---|---|
| Romanian deadlift | 3 × 8-10 |
| Overhead press | 3 × 6-8 |
| Lat pulldown or pull-up | 3 × 8-10 |
| Bulgarian split squat | 2 × 8-10 per leg |
| Dumbbell curl | 2 × 10-12 |
| Standing calf raise | 3 × 10-15 |

**Day C**

| Exercise | Sets × reps |
|---|---|
| Leg press | 3 × 10-12 |
| Incline dumbbell press | 3 × 8-10 |
| Seated cable row | 3 × 10-12 |
| Seated leg curl | 2 × 10-12 |
| Cable fly | 2 × 12-15 |
| Lateral raise | 2 × 12-15 |

That's 15-16 working sets a session. Once you've run it for a couple of months and recovery is fine, add a fourth set to the first three lifts of each day. That takes chest, back and quads to 10-12 sets a week without adding an exercise.

![A lifter pressing a pair of dumbbells on a flat bench in a bright gym](/blog/3-day-workout-split/dumbbell-press.webp)
*Photo: [Andrea Piacquadio](https://www.pexels.com/photo/man-in-white-tank-top-and-grey-shorts-lifting-weights-3839310/) on Pexels*

One catch: every session has legs in it. If you hate leg training, full body means you never get a day off from it. Your legs will be better for it, but know that going in.

## Option 2: Upper/lower/full

Upper/lower/full gives you two focused days and one mixed day. Every muscle gets hit twice a week, and the sessions feel more like a "proper" split, which some people simply enjoy more. Enjoying it counts, since you'll be doing this for months.

**Monday: Upper**

| Exercise | Sets × reps |
|---|---|
| Bench press | 3 × 6-8 |
| Barbell row | 3 × 6-8 |
| Seated dumbbell shoulder press | 3 × 8-10 |
| Lat pulldown | 3 × 8-10 |
| Lateral raise | 2 × 12-15 |
| Dumbbell curl | 2 × 10-12 |
| Triceps pushdown | 2 × 10-12 |

**Wednesday: Lower**

| Exercise | Sets × reps |
|---|---|
| Back squat | 3 × 5-8 |
| Romanian deadlift | 3 × 8-10 |
| Leg press | 3 × 10-12 |
| Lying leg curl | 3 × 10-12 |
| Standing calf raise | 3 × 10-15 |
| Cable crunch | 2 × 10-15 |

**Friday: Full body**

| Exercise | Sets × reps |
|---|---|
| Bulgarian split squat | 3 × 8-10 per leg |
| Incline dumbbell press | 3 × 8-10 |
| Pull-up or chest-supported row | 3 × 8-10 |
| Seated leg curl | 2 × 10-12 |
| Lateral raise | 2 × 12-15 |
| Overhead triceps extension | 2 × 10-12 |

Put the full-body day last. It's the lightest of the three, and on Friday you'll appreciate that.

## Option 3: Push/pull/legs once a week

The 3-day PPL is a popular 3 day gym split, and it's the one we'd recommend least. Each muscle gets one session a week, so if you miss a day, that muscle goes two weeks untrained. To reach decent weekly volume, each session has to be long.

That said, it works for experienced lifters who enjoy it and know how to push hard sets, and it's a fine stopgap during a busy month. If you run it, accept 18-21 sets a session.

**Push**

| Exercise | Sets × reps |
|---|---|
| Bench press | 4 × 6-8 |
| Overhead press | 3 × 6-8 |
| Incline dumbbell press | 3 × 8-10 |
| Cable fly | 2 × 12-15 |
| Lateral raise | 4 × 12-15 |
| Triceps pushdown | 3 × 10-12 |
| Overhead triceps extension | 2 × 10-12 |

**Pull**

| Exercise | Sets × reps |
|---|---|
| Pull-up or lat pulldown | 4 × 6-10 |
| Barbell row | 3 × 6-8 |
| Seated cable row | 3 × 10-12 |
| Face pull | 3 × 12-15 |
| EZ-bar curl | 3 × 8-10 |
| Hammer curl | 2 × 10-12 |

**Legs**

| Exercise | Sets × reps |
|---|---|
| Back squat | 4 × 5-8 |
| Romanian deadlift | 3 × 8-10 |
| Leg press | 3 × 10-12 |
| Lying leg curl | 3 × 10-12 |
| Leg extension | 2 × 12-15 |
| Standing calf raise | 4 × 10-15 |

Leg day once a week means leg day is very, very long. If you can train more often, run the same three days on a rolling push, pull, legs, rest cycle. Each muscle then comes round every four days instead of every seven, and you don't change a single exercise. The [push/pull/legs pillar](/blog/push-pull-legs-routine) covers that set-up.

## Option 4: FBEOD (full body every other day)

FBEOD stands for full body every other day. You alternate two workouts, A and B, with one rest day between every session, regardless of the weekday. Over two weeks that's seven sessions, or 3.5 a week, which is why the FBEOD split sits halfway between a 3-day and a 4-day plan.

Because you train more often, each session is shorter: five or six exercises and 13-15 sets.

**FBEOD workout A**

| Exercise | Sets × reps |
|---|---|
| Back squat | 3 × 5-8 |
| Bench press | 3 × 6-8 |
| Barbell row | 3 × 8-10 |
| Lying leg curl | 2 × 10-12 |
| Lateral raise | 2 × 12-15 |

**FBEOD workout B**

| Exercise | Sets × reps |
|---|---|
| Romanian deadlift | 3 × 8-10 |
| Overhead press | 3 × 6-8 |
| Lat pulldown or pull-up | 3 × 8-10 |
| Leg press | 2 × 10-12 |
| Dumbbell curl | 2 × 10-12 |
| Triceps pushdown | 2 × 10-12 |

Calves are left out to keep sessions short, and chest is the lightest muscle in this plan. If either lags, add 3 sets of calf raises or 2 sets of dips to workout B.

The downside is that the weekday moves. Your heavy squat lands on Monday one week and Tuesday the next, which is great if your week has no fixed shape (shift work, college, travel) and annoying if Thursday is always football.

## Mon/Wed/Fri vs rolling every other day

Both schedules give you a rest day after every session. They differ in what happens at the weekend.

![A hand writing on a wall calendar with a marker while planning the week](/blog/3-day-workout-split/calendar.webp)
*Photo: [RDNE Stock project](https://www.pexels.com/photo/woman-writing-in-calendar-8581118/) on Pexels*

**Fixed Mon/Wed/Fri** (or Tue/Thu/Sat, or Sun/Tue/Thu):

| Week | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A | Rest | B | Rest | C | Rest | Rest |
| 2 | A | Rest | B | Rest | C | Rest | Rest |

**Rolling FBEOD:**

| Week | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A | Rest | B | Rest | A | Rest | B |
| 2 | Rest | A | Rest | B | Rest | A | Rest |
| 3 | B | Rest | A | Rest | B | Rest | A |

| | Mon/Wed/Fri | Rolling every other day |
|---|---|---|
| Sessions in a 30-day month | About 13 | 15 |
| Rest between sessions | 48 h, plus 72 h over the weekend | Always 48 h |
| Fits a 9-to-5 week | Very well | Clashes some weeks |
| Missed-session buffer | Saturday | None built in |
| Remembering what's next | Easy, the weekday tells you | You need a log |

Mon/Wed/Fri has one quiet advantage. If you miss Wednesday, you train Thursday and push Friday's session to Saturday, and the next week starts as normal. On a rolling schedule, you simply train the next workout in order. Our post on [what to do when you miss a day](/blog/missed-a-day-push-pull-legs) covers both cases.

The calendar won't remember whether today is A or B on a rolling plan, so something has to. In [GymLedger](/), the dashboard puts the next workout in your rotation at the top, so you open the app and start it in one tap, whatever the weekday.

### Can you train three days in a row?

You can, but it depends on the split. PPL on Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday works because each day trains different muscles. Full body three days running means your legs face heavy squats again 24 hours after the last session, which most people can't recover from well. If back-to-back days are your only option, pick PPL or upper/lower/full with the full-body day after a rest day. How long different muscles need between sessions is covered in our guide to [muscle recovery time](/blog/how-long-does-it-take-for-muscles-to-recover).

## Which 3 day split workout should you pick?

Use the table below and pick the first row that fits you.

| If you are... | Pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| In your first 6-12 months of lifting | Full body x3 | Squat, press and pull practice three times a week |
| Busy, with fixed workdays | Full body x3 or upper/lower/full, Mon/Wed/Fri | Predictable, with a weekend buffer |
| Someone who likes a dedicated leg day | Upper/lower/full | Focused days, still twice a week per muscle |
| Experienced and you love PPL | PPL x1, or rolling PPL | Fine if you can handle 70+ minute sessions |
| A shift worker or student with no fixed week | FBEOD | Your rest days set the schedule, whatever the weekday |
| Regularly finding a fourth free day | Move to 4 days | See our [4-day workout split](/blog/4-day-workout-split) |

The American College of Sports Medicine's [position stand on resistance training](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/) suggests 2-3 days a week for novices and 3-4 for intermediates. So three days isn't a "beginner only" plan. Plenty of lifters with years of training still run three full-body days and keep progressing.

## How to progress on a 3 day split workout

Once the split is set, results come from adding weight or reps over time. The simplest system is [double progression](/blog/double-progression): keep the weight the same until you hit the top of the rep range on every set, then add the smallest jump your gym allows.

Here's a back squat on full-body Day A, 3 × 5-8, trained once a week:

| Week | kg | lb | Reps | Next step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 60 kg | 135 lb | 8, 7, 6 | Same weight |
| 2 | 60 kg | 135 lb | 8, 8, 7 | Same weight |
| 3 | 60 kg | 135 lb | 8, 8, 8 | **Add 2.5 kg (5 lb)** |
| 4 | 62.5 kg | 140 lb | 7, 6, 6 | Same weight |
| 5 | 62.5 kg | 140 lb | 8, 7, 7 | Same weight |
| 6 | 62.5 kg | 140 lb | 8, 8, 8 | **Add 2.5 kg (5 lb)** |

That's 5 kg (10 lb) on the squat in six weeks, at full reps. Complete beginners can often skip the rep build-up and add 2.5 kg (5 lb) to each main lift every week for their first couple of months. Once that stalls, switch to the table above.

![A lifter setting up for a barbell deadlift with a loaded bar in a gym](/blog/3-day-workout-split/deadlift.webp)
*Photo: [Luke Miller](https://www.pexels.com/photo/man-weightlifting-at-gym-15373908/) on Pexels*

A few rules that keep a 3-day plan moving:

1. Change one thing at a time. Add weight or reps and leave the exercise list alone for at least 6-8 weeks, so the numbers stay comparable.
2. Add sets before you add days. When progress slows and recovery is good, add one set to the first three lifts of each session before you think about a fourth day.
3. Take a lighter week every 6-10 weeks, or sooner if several lifts stall at once and your joints are complaining.
4. Log every working set. With only three sessions a week, you can't afford to waste one guessing what you lifted last time.

## Putting it into practice

1. **Pick your split** from the decision table. When in doubt, full body x3.
2. **Pick your days.** Mon/Wed/Fri if your week is fixed, every other day if it isn't.
3. **Copy the workouts** above and keep them unchanged for 6-8 weeks.
4. **Start light enough** that your first session lands in the middle of each rep range.
5. **Use double progression** on every lift and record every set.
6. **Review after 6-8 weeks.** If your main lifts are up, keep going. If not, add a set to lagging muscles, check your sleep and food, or look at a [custom split](/blog/how-to-build-your-own-workout-split) that fits your week better.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a 3 day split enough to build muscle?

Yes. Three sessions a week is enough to give each muscle 8-12 or more hard sets, which covers what most beginners and intermediates need to grow. Research comparing frequencies finds that weekly volume matters more than how many days you train when sets are matched. Consistency matters most, and three sessions a week are easier to keep than five.

### Is full body or push/pull/legs better for 3 days a week?

For most people, full body. It trains each muscle three times a week, keeps sessions shorter and means a missed day doesn't leave any muscle untrained for two weeks. Push/pull/legs once a week can work for experienced lifters who enjoy it, but each session has to be long to reach good weekly volume. Upper/lower/full sits between the two at twice a week.

### What is an FBEOD split?

FBEOD means full body every other day. You alternate two full-body workouts, A and B, with one rest day after each session no matter what weekday it is. Over two weeks that's seven sessions, about 3.5 a week. It suits people with irregular schedules, such as shift workers and students, but the training days move around, so it can clash with fixed weekly commitments.

### Can I do a 3 day split on consecutive days?

You can with push/pull/legs, because each day trains different muscles, so Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday is workable. Full body on three straight days is a poor idea, since your legs and back face heavy work again after only 24 hours. If consecutive days are your only option, use PPL or put an upper and a lower day back to back, then do the full-body day after a rest.

### How long should a 3 day split workout take?

Most full-body and upper/lower/full sessions take 50-70 minutes with 2-3 minutes of rest on big lifts and about 90 seconds on small ones. A once-a-week PPL session usually runs 65-80 minutes because it packs a whole week's work for those muscles into one day. If you regularly run over 90 minutes, trim a set or two from the last exercises rather than rushing your rest.
