If you want a **Hevy alternative without social feed** features, the strongest options as of October 2026 are Strong, Setgraph, FitNotes 2, Liftosaur, SensAI and GymLedger. None of them describe a follow-and-like feed on their official pages or App Store listings. They differ in platforms, how they charge, and how much they try to do beyond logging sets. Below is a fair, side-by-side look so you can pick a private workout tracker that fits how you train.

## First, credit where it's due: Hevy is a good app

Hevy is popular for real reasons. According to its [official site](https://www.hevyapp.com/), it is a free workout tracker for iOS and Android, with apps for Apple Watch and Wear OS, plus a web view of your routines and progress. It covers the logging basics well: warm-up, drop and failure set markers, a routine planner, rest timers, exercise notes, charts, personal records and one-rep-max calculations.

Its social layer is a feature, not a flaw. The site describes following other athletes, liking and commenting on workouts, and saving other people's routines. Plenty of lifters love that. A training partner who sees your session and leaves a comment can be the push you need to show up on a Tuesday night.

It's also worth knowing that Hevy gives you some control. Its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hevy-workout-tracker-gym-log/id1458862350) says you can set your profile as private or public. If your only concern is strangers seeing your numbers, that setting may be enough, and you may not need to switch at all.

## Why some lifters want a workout app with no social media layer

So why go looking for Hevy alternatives? The reasons we hear most often are not about Hevy being bad. They're about what a social layer does to some people's training.

- **Comparison creep.** Seeing someone's 180 kg (397 lb) deadlift right before your 100 kg (220 lb) session can make a solid day feel small. Some lifters train better when the only benchmark is their own last workout.
- **Focus.** A feed is designed to be scrolled. Between sets, that can turn a 2-minute rest into 5 minutes of scrolling. A quiet app keeps you on the plan.
- **Privacy.** Body weight, injuries and training history are personal. Some people simply don't want an account that exists partly to be seen by others.
- **Simplicity.** If you only need sets, reps, weight and a rest timer, every extra tab is noise.

None of these are universal. If the community motivates you, keep it. But if you've noticed yourself opening a workout app and ending up somewhere other than your log, a private workout tracker is worth trying.

## The alternatives, one by one

### Strong

Strong is one of the longest-running workout logging apps, and its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/strong-workout-tracker-gym-log/id464254577) shows why it's a common pick among Hevy alternatives. It runs on iPhone and has a full Apple Watch app that can log workouts with or without your phone. The [official site](https://www.strong.app/) also lists Android.

On features, the App Store listing mentions a plate calculator, a warm-up calculator, an auto countdown rest timer (with Live Activity and Dynamic Island support added in a recent version), volume and 1RM charts, personal records, and CSV export. Sets can be tagged as warm-up, failure or drop sets. The site lists supersets and RPE too.

**Social layer:** the listing says the share sheet makes it easy to share routines and workouts with friends. We found no description of a feed, followers or likes.

**Pricing model:** free to download. The free version saves unlimited workouts but limits you to 3 custom routines. Strong PRO is offered as a subscription, and the in-app purchase list also includes a "Forever" option.

**Trade-offs to know:** if you run more than three routines (a typical push/pull/legs plus upper/lower rotation, for example), you'll likely want PRO. It's a dense app, which power users love and some beginners find busy.

### Setgraph

Setgraph pitches itself on speed. Its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/setgraph-workout-log/id1209781676) describes logging sets in a few taps, rest timers that start automatically and show on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, and a "Smart Plates" feature that totals the weight on the bar. It runs on iPhone, Apple Watch and Apple Vision.

The [official site](https://setgraph.app/) adds a workout planner, a log that pulls from your history so you can see what you did last time, progress charts, and an AI workout generator that builds plans around your goals and equipment. The App Store listing mentions supersets and circuits, a choice of 1RM formulas, Apple Health sync and CSV export.

**Social layer:** you can share workout folders by link, and create share cards you can post anywhere. No feed, followers or comments are described.

**Pricing model:** free with in-app purchases. A Pro Membership is offered monthly, yearly or as a lifetime purchase, and a free trial is listed.

**Trade-offs to know:** it's Apple-only, so it won't help if you or your training partner use Android. The AI plan generator is a plus or a minus depending on whether you want the app to suggest training or simply record it.

### FitNotes 2

FitNotes started as a well-loved Android logger. [FitNotes 2](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fitnotes-2-gym-workout-log/id1538896016) is the iOS app, which its listing describes as based on the original FitNotes. It runs on iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch and Apple Vision, and it can import the original FitNotes database and CSV files.

It's built for lifters who like data. The listing includes routines, automatic personal records, 1RM and percentage calculators, a plate calculator with per-gym profiles, body measurements with HealthKit sync, goals and graphs, RPE and RIR, warm-up sets, several interval timers, iCloud backups and CSV import and export.

**Social layer:** the listing says no account is needed, and you can share workouts as text or images. No community features are described. For a workout app with no social media angle at all, this is about as quiet as it gets.

**Pricing model:** free with in-app purchases. The free version saves up to 12 workouts, so you'd need to delete older sessions or pay to keep a full history. Paid options unlock unlimited use.

**Trade-offs to know:** that 12-workout cap means the free tier is really a trial for anyone who trains regularly. The feature list is long, which is great if you want it and a lot of screens if you don't.

### Liftosaur

[Liftosaur](https://www.liftosaur.com/) takes a different approach. It's built around programs and progression rules, and it has its own text-based scripting language, Liftoscript, for writing them. If you've ever wanted your app to know that "add 2.5 kg (5 lb) when I hit 3x8, deload 10% after two misses," this is the kind of tool that does it.

It's available on iOS, Android and the web, with a separate web-based program editor. The site lists built-in programs (including GZCLP, 5/3/1 for Beginners, Starting Strength and Texas Method), automatic linear and double progression, per-exercise graphs, weekly volume per muscle group, 1RM and RPE-based loading, a plate calculator and body measurements.

**Social layer:** you can generate a link to share a program, and the community gathers on Reddit and Discord. We found no in-app feed described.

**Pricing model:** a free tier plus premium features. The site doesn't list prices.

**Trade-offs to know:** the power comes from scripting, and that has a learning curve. If you just want to type in today's sets and go, it may be more than you need. If you follow a structured program like the ones covered in our [double progression guide](/blog/double-progression), it can automate a lot.

### SensAI

[SensAI](https://www.sensai.fit/) isn't a pure logger. It's an AI personal trainer app: it builds weekly programs, adjusts them based on your history, and uses data from Apple Health and connected wearables such as Apple Watch, Garmin, Oura and WHOOP to set daily intensity. It also tracks sets with rest timers and works offline.

**Social layer:** the official page doesn't describe any social features.

**Pricing model:** the page says it's free to download with no credit card required. It doesn't describe paid tiers, so check the App Store listing for current details.

**Trade-offs to know:** it's iOS only (iOS 17 or later per the site). And it's a coach, not a quiet log. If you write your own program and just want to record it, the AI side may feel like it's in the way. If you want guidance, that's the point.

### GymLedger

[GymLedger](/) is our app, so here's the honest version. It's an iPhone-only workout tracker with no social features, by design. There's no feed, no followers, no likes and no ads. Your data isn't sold or shared, and you can delete your account and data from inside the app.

What it does: you build custom workout templates (pick exercises, sets and rep ranges) for any split, whether that's push/pull/legs, upper/lower, full body or something you made up. The home screen shows your last workout and today's pick, so you can start in one tap or choose a different session. Logging sets, reps and weight is fast, and you can choose kg or lbs. The rest timer has haptic feedback, keeps counting on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, and lets you skip rest when you're ready. You get per-exercise strength progress charts, personal bests, and body-weight tracking with charts. It syncs across your iPhones, supports Sign in with Apple, Google or email, and is available in 12 languages, including Hindi.

**Social layer:** none. That's the product decision, not a missing feature.

**Pricing model:** GymLedger requires a purchase to unlock. New subscribers get a 7-day free trial on the monthly subscription, or you can buy a one-time Lifetime unlock.

**Trade-offs to know:**

- **iPhone only.** No Android and no Apple Watch app.
- **No free tier after the trial.** If you want a free-forever logger, Strong's free version or Liftosaur's free tier is a better fit.
- **Deliberately simple.** It doesn't have RPE or RIR fields, supersets, a plate calculator, 1RM estimates or data export. If those matter to you, Strong, Setgraph or FitNotes 2 cover more ground.
- **No social features at all.** If you'd occasionally like to share a session with a friend, the share options in Strong or Setgraph may suit you better.

## How to choose a Hevy alternative without social feed distractions

Every app above is a reasonable pick. The right one depends on a few honest questions.

### 1. What phone do you (and your training partner) use?

If you're on Android, your options from this list are Strong and Liftosaur. If you're on iPhone, all six work. If you switch between platforms, Liftosaur's web app and Strong's Android and iPhone apps are worth a look.

### 2. Do you want a log, a program engine, or a coach?

- **A log:** Strong, Setgraph, FitNotes 2 or GymLedger. You decide what to do; the app records it.
- **A program engine:** Liftosaur. You define the rules; the app applies them.
- **A coach:** SensAI. The app suggests what to do.

### 3. How much do you care about advanced metrics?

If you track RPE, RIR, estimated 1RM or want a plate calculator, Strong, Setgraph, FitNotes 2 and Liftosaur all mention some of these. If you mostly care about seeing your top sets climb over time, a simpler app with clean per-exercise charts is enough. Our guide on [how to track progressive overload](/blog/how-to-track-progressive-overload) covers what numbers actually matter.

### 4. How do you prefer to pay?

- **Free with limits:** Strong (3 custom routines), FitNotes 2 (12 saved workouts), Liftosaur (premium features locked).
- **Subscription or lifetime:** Strong, Setgraph, FitNotes 2 and GymLedger all list one-time or lifetime options alongside or instead of subscriptions.
- **Try before buying:** Setgraph and GymLedger list free trials.

### 5. Do you want sharing, just not a feed?

There's a middle ground. Strong, Setgraph, FitNotes 2 and Liftosaur let you send a workout, routine or program to a friend without having a public profile. GymLedger and SensAI don't describe sharing at all. And remember, Hevy itself offers a private profile option.

## What to look for in any private workout tracker

Whatever you pick, test these in your first week:

1. **Logging speed mid-set.** Can you record a set of 8 at 60 kg (132 lb) in a couple of taps with sweaty hands? If not, you'll stop logging.
2. **Templates that match your split.** You should be able to build your own routine, not just pick from a library. If you're still designing one, see [how to build your own workout split](/blog/how-to-build-your-own-workout-split).
3. **Last-session visibility.** Seeing what you lifted last time is what makes progressive overload work.
4. **Units you actually use.** If your gym has kg plates, log in kg. Mixing units makes charts messy.
5. **A rest timer you can see from the Lock Screen.** Several apps here mention Lock Screen or Dynamic Island timers.
6. **Data controls.** Check whether you can delete your account and data, and whether the app can export it if that matters to you.

## Putting it into practice

Pick one app, not three. Install it, enter your current routine, and log every session for two weeks. Two weeks is enough to know whether the logging flow fits how you train and whether the app helps you lift a little more than last time.

If you're leaving Hevy, write down your current working weights for your main lifts before you switch. A short list like "bench 70 kg x 8 (154 lb x 8), squat 100 kg x 5 (220 lb x 5)" is all you need to start strong in a new app. Then let the quiet do its job: open the app, log the set, rest, repeat.

If you decide a Hevy alternative without social feed noise is right for you and you're on iPhone, GymLedger is one option to try during its free trial. If you need Android, an Apple Watch app or a free-forever tier, one of the other apps above will serve you better, and that's fine.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a Hevy alternative without social feed features?

Yes. As of October 2026, Strong, Setgraph, FitNotes 2, Liftosaur, SensAI and GymLedger don't describe a follow-and-like feed on their official pages or App Store listings. Some let you share a workout or program by link or share sheet, which is different from a public feed. Check each app's current listing, since features change over time.

### Can I make my Hevy account private instead of switching apps?

Hevy's App Store listing says you can set your profile as private or public. If your main concern is other people seeing your workouts, that setting may solve it without moving your data. If the issue is the feed itself pulling your attention between sets, a workout app with no social media layer may suit you better.

### What is the best free private workout tracker?

It depends on your limits. Strong's free version saves unlimited workouts but caps custom routines at three. FitNotes 2 is free for up to 12 saved workouts. Liftosaur has a free tier with some premium features locked. SensAI is free to download. GymLedger and Setgraph offer free trials before purchase rather than a permanent free tier.

### Is GymLedger available on Android?

No. GymLedger is iPhone only, and it does not have an Apple Watch app. If you use Android, Strong and Liftosaur both list Android apps on their official sites, and neither describes a social feed. GymLedger syncs across your iPhones and works in kg or lbs, with Sign in with Apple, Google or email.

### Do I lose my training history if I switch from Hevy?

Your history stays in Hevy unless you delete it, but moving it into another app depends on each app's import options, which vary. A simple approach is to note your current working weights and reps for your main lifts, start fresh in the new app, and keep Hevy installed for reference until you no longer need it.
