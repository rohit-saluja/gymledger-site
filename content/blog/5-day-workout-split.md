The best 5 day workout split for most natural lifters is **PPLUL**: push, pull, legs, upper, lower, Monday to Friday with the weekend off. It trains every muscle twice a week and keeps sessions around an hour. The classic **bro split** (one body part a day) still builds muscle, but it hits the big muscles once a week and crams 15-20 sets into one session. The **Arnold split** (chest and back, shoulders and arms, legs) is a six-day plan at heart; on five days it rotates, so each muscle comes round about 1.7 times a week.

This guide gives you all four options with full workouts, a weekly sets comparison, schedules, recovery rules, and a list of who shouldn't train five days at all. If you're coming from push/pull/legs, our [push/pull/legs routine guide](/blog/push-pull-legs-routine) covers the 3- and 6-day versions and the rolling 5-day PPL.

## The 5 day workout split options at a glance

| Split | Typical week | Big muscles trained | Sets per session | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PPLUL | Push, pull, legs, upper, lower | 2x a week | 16-17 | Most intermediates |
| Upper/lower + PPL hybrids | ULPPL, or upper/lower x2 + an arms and delts day | 2x a week | 16-20 | Lifters who want a mid-week rest or a weak-point day |
| Bro split | Chest, back, shoulders, legs, arms | 1x a week | 15-20 | Lifters who love one-muscle days and rarely miss |
| Arnold split (5-day rotation) | Chest/back, shoulders/arms, legs, rotating | About 1.7x a week | 18-20 | Experienced lifters who want big upper-body days |

"Big muscles" here means chest, back, quads and hamstrings. Smaller muscles get extra indirect work on every split, especially on the bro split.

## Option 1: PPLUL (push, pull, legs, upper, lower)

The PPLUL split takes three push/pull/legs days and adds an upper and a lower day. Every muscle gets two sessions a week, and you get two days completely off. We'd pick this for most people who have five days and can recover from them.

### PPLUL schedule

| | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standard | Push | Pull | Legs | Upper | Lower | Rest | Rest |
| More recovery | Push | Pull | Legs | Rest | Upper | Lower | Rest |

The second version adds a rest day on Thursday, so your legs get 72 hours between Wednesday and Saturday. If heavy deadlifts two days after squats leave your lower back cranky, use it.

The PPL days below use moderate reps for volume. The upper and lower days use heavier, lower-rep compounds, so you get both kinds of work in one week.

| Incline dumbbell press | 3 × 8-12 |

| Overhead triceps extension | 3 × 10-12 |

## Option 2: Upper/lower and PPL hybrids

PPLUL is one hybrid. Two others are worth knowing, because they fix two common complaints about it: no rest day in the middle of the week, and no room for a lagging body part.

| Hybrid | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ULPPL | Upper | Lower | Rest | Push | Pull | Legs | Rest |
| Upper/lower x2 + weak-point day | Upper | Lower | Rest | Upper | Lower | Arms and delts | Rest |
| Rolling PPL | Push | Pull | Legs | Rest | Push | Pull | Rest, then carry on |

**ULPPL** uses the same five workouts as PPLUL in a different order. The heavy upper and lower days come first in the week while you're fresh, there's a rest day on Wednesday, and the two leg sessions sit four days apart.

**Upper/lower x2 plus a weak-point day** is the easiest way to grow a 4-day plan into five. Keep your [4-day upper/lower workouts](/blog/4-day-workout-split) exactly as they are and add a short fifth session for whatever lags. For most people that's arms and side delts:

| Exercise | Sets × reps |
|---|---|
| Cable lateral raise | 4 × 12-20 |
| Rear delt fly | 3 × 15-20 |
| EZ-bar curl | 3 × 8-12 |
| Overhead triceps extension | 3 × 10-12 |
| Incline dumbbell curl | 2 × 10-15 |
| Triceps pushdown | 2 × 10-15 |

That adds 7 delt sets and 5 sets each for biceps and triceps to your week, in a session that rarely runs past 45 minutes. It's also the easiest day to skip without harm when life gets busy.

**Rolling PPL** trains five days a week and always does the next workout in order, whatever the weekday, so each muscle gets about 1.7 sessions a week. The [push/pull/legs pillar](/blog/push-pull-legs-routine) has the full rotation table.

## Option 3: The bro split

The bro split gives each body part its own day: chest, back, shoulders, legs, arms. It's the classic bodybuilding-magazine layout, and plenty of people still love it because every session has one clear job.

### Bro split schedule

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chest | Back | Shoulders | Legs | Arms | Rest | Rest |

This order spaces out the pressing days (Monday, Wednesday, Friday) and puts 72 hours between back day and arm day, so your biceps aren't already tired when you curl.

### The case for and against the bro split for natural lifters

The internet has decided the bro split is useless for natural lifters. The research is less dramatic.

- A 2016 meta-analysis found that, with weekly sets matched, training a muscle twice a week grew more muscle than once a week ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27102172/)).
- A larger 2019 analysis of 25 studies found no significant difference between frequencies when weekly volume was equal ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30558493/)).
- A 2015 study in 20 trained men compared a split routine (each muscle once a week) with full-body training three times a week. Strength gains were similar, and the full-body group gained more muscle thickness at one of the three sites measured (the forearm flexors) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25932981/)).
- A 2024 meta-analysis of 14 studies comparing split and full-body routines found similar strength and muscle growth when volume was matched ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38595233/)).

So a bro split with enough hard sets will build muscle. The usual argument against it for naturals, that growth signals fade within a couple of days so you need to hit a muscle again sooner, is plausible, but the volume-matched studies show a small gap at most.

The bigger problems are practical.

1. Session quality drops. Fifteen chest sets in a row means the last five are done with tired triceps and a fading bench, while two sessions of seven or eight give every set a fresh start.
2. Missed days hurt more. Skip Thursday and your legs go two weeks without training.
3. Leg day becomes an event. Twenty sets for legs in one go is the session people quietly skip.

Critics also overstate the once-a-week problem. Triceps work on chest, shoulder and arm days, front delts on chest and shoulder days, and biceps on back and arm days, so the once-a-week label really only fits chest, back, quads and hamstrings.

If you love the bro split, keep it. Run it with 10-15 hard sets per muscle, take most sets close to failure, and don't miss leg day. To add frequency without losing the format, turn Friday's arm day into an "upper pump" day with 3-4 sets each for chest and back, then the arm work.

## Option 4: The Arnold split on five days

The Arnold split is the routine Arnold Schwarzenegger made famous: chest and back together, shoulders and arms together, then legs, with the cycle run twice a week. Pairing chest with back lets you alternate a press and a pull, which saves time and keeps you warm between sets.

The original runs six days and was built for full-time competitive bodybuilders. On five days you rotate the three workouts, so the weekday changes:

| Week | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Chest/back | Shoulders/arms | Legs | Chest/back | Shoulders/arms |
| 2 | Legs | Chest/back | Shoulders/arms | Legs | Chest/back |
| 3 | Shoulders/arms | Legs | Chest/back | Shoulders/arms | Legs |

Every workout comes round five times in three weeks, or about 1.7 times a week.

**Chest and back**

| Exercise | Sets × reps |
|---|---|
| Bench press | 4 × 6-8 |
| Pull-up or lat pulldown | 4 × 6-10 |
| Incline dumbbell press | 3 × 8-10 |
| Barbell or chest-supported row | 3 × 8-10 |
| Cable fly | 2 × 12-15 |
| Dumbbell pullover | 2 × 10-12 |

**Shoulders and arms**

| Exercise | Sets × reps |
|---|---|
| Seated dumbbell press or Arnold press | 3 × 8-10 |
| Dumbbell lateral raise | 3 × 12-15 |
| Rear delt fly | 3 × 12-15 |
| EZ-bar curl | 3 × 8-10 |
| Close-grip bench press or dips | 3 × 8-10 |
| Incline dumbbell curl | 2 × 10-12 |
| Overhead triceps extension | 2 × 10-12 |

**Legs**

| Exercise | Sets × reps |
|---|---|
| Back squat | 4 × 6-8 |
| Romanian deadlift | 3 × 8-10 |
| Walking lunge or leg press | 3 × 10-12 |
| Lying leg curl | 3 × 10-12 |
| Standing calf raise | 4 × 10-15 |
| Hanging leg raise | 3 × 10-15 |

Watch the overlap. Shoulders and arms comes the day after chest and back, so your front delts and triceps press again 24 hours after bench, and your biceps curl after a day of pull-ups. If that leaves your elbows sore, put a rest day after chest and back. If you go up to the full six days, drop a set from most exercises, or chest, back and shoulders each reach 18 sets a week.

## Weekly sets per muscle: how the splits compare

These are direct hard sets per week from the workouts above. Deadlifts aren't counted, since they train back, glutes and hamstrings at once. Arms also get plenty of indirect work from presses and rows.

| Muscle | PPLUL | Bro split | Arnold (5-day rotation) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chest | 11 | 15 | About 15 |
| Back | 14 | 12 | About 15 |
| Shoulders | 12 | 14 | About 15 |
| Quads | 11 | 10 | About 12 |
| Hamstrings | 9 | 6 | About 10 |
| Biceps (direct) | 7 | 9 | About 8 |
| Triceps (direct) | 5 | 9 | About 8 |
| Calves | 6 | 4 | About 7 |
| Big muscles per week | 2x | 1x | About 1.7x |
| Longest session | 17 sets | 20 sets | 20 sets |

All three land in the 10-20 sets a week range for most muscles, which is where most intermediates grow well. Our guide to [how many sets per muscle group per week](/blog/how-many-sets-per-muscle-group-per-week) explains how to set your own numbers. The difference is how the sets are delivered. PPLUL spreads each muscle's sets over two sessions, while the bro split puts the whole week into one.

## Recovery on a 5 day split

Five sessions leave you two rest days, so plan your recovery as carefully as your workouts.

- Space the overlaps. Check which muscles get trained twice and how far apart. On PPLUL, legs come on Wednesday and Friday and pressing on Monday and Thursday, with at least one day between each pair.
- Leave reps in the tank. Finish most sets with 1-3 good reps left. Going to failure on every set of every session is the fastest way to make a fifth day feel like a punishment.
- Plan lighter weeks. Every 6-8 weeks, or when several lifts stall at once, cut your sets roughly in half for a week. Our guide to the [deload week](/blog/deload-week) shows how.
- Watch for warning signs. Lifts going backwards for two weeks, joints that ache at rest and worsening sleep all mean it's time to drop volume. If a joint still hurts after a week or two of lighter training, get it checked by a doctor or physio.

## Who should not run a 5 day workout split

A fifth day only helps if you can recover from it and actually turn up. Skip it if any of these sound like you:

- **You're in your first 6-12 months of lifting.** The American College of Sports Medicine's [position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/) suggests 2-3 training days a week for novices, 3-4 for intermediates and 4-5 for advanced lifters. A beginner on three full-body days will progress just as fast with less fatigue. Start with our [3-day split guide](/blog/3-day-workout-split).
- **You regularly miss sessions.** A 5-day plan that keeps turning into three days leaves gaps, especially on a bro split. A 4-day plan you actually complete beats it.
- **You're stalling on four days.** If your lifts haven't moved in a month, a fifth day rarely fixes it. Sleep, food and effort usually do.
- **Your job is physical or your sleep is short.** Warehouse shifts, new babies and 5-hour nights all eat into recovery that the extra session needs.
- **You play a sport or run a lot.** Two hard conditioning days plus five lifting days is a lot of weekly stress. Drop to four lifting days in season.

If any of those fit, the [4-day workout split](/blog/4-day-workout-split) gives you close to the same weekly volume with an extra rest day.

## How to progress on a 5 day split

On PPLUL you train most muscles twice a week with different exercises, so each lift progresses on its own track. Use [double progression](/blog/double-progression): stay at a weight until you reach the top of the rep range on every set, then add the smallest jump.

Here are the two chest presses from PPLUL over six weeks. Monday's flat bench is 3 × 6-8 and Thursday's incline bench is 3 × 5-8.

| Week | Monday bench press | Thursday incline bench press |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 70 kg (155 lb): 8, 7, 7 | 60 kg (135 lb): 7, 6, 5 |
| 2 | 70 kg (155 lb): 8, 8, 7 | 60 kg (135 lb): 8, 7, 6 |
| 3 | 70 kg (155 lb): 8, 8, 8 | 60 kg (135 lb): 8, 7, 7 |
| 4 | 72.5 kg (160 lb): 7, 6, 6 | 60 kg (135 lb): 8, 8, 8 |
| 5 | 72.5 kg (160 lb): 7, 7, 6 | 62.5 kg (140 lb): 6, 6, 5 |
| 6 | 72.5 kg (160 lb): 8, 7, 7 | 62.5 kg (140 lb): 7, 6, 6 |

The two lifts earn their weight jumps in different weeks, and that's fine. Judge each one against its own last session.

For isolation lifts like lateral raises and curls, build reps first and only jump weight when you're at the top of the range on every set. A jump from 8 kg to 10 kg dumbbells (or 15 lb to 20 lb) is a 25-33% increase, so expect your reps to drop well down the range.

## Putting it into practice

1. **Check that a 5 day workout split suits you.** If you're new, often miss sessions or are stalling on four, stay on fewer days.
2. **Pick a split.** PPLUL for most people, a 4+1 hybrid if you're growing a 4-day plan, the bro split only if you love it and rarely miss, the Arnold split if you want big upper-body days and recover well.
3. **Set your week** from the schedule tables, keeping a rest day where your overlaps need one.
4. **Run the workouts unchanged for 6-8 weeks** with double progression on every lift.
5. **Take a lighter week** every 6-8 weeks or when several lifts stall.
6. **Review your logs.** Add 2-3 sets to lagging muscles if recovery is good, or cut sets if joints and sleep say otherwise. Our guide to [building your own workout split](/blog/how-to-build-your-own-workout-split) walks through those adjustments.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a 5 day workout split good for building muscle?

Yes, for intermediate and advanced lifters who can recover from it. Five sessions make it easy to reach 10-20 hard sets per muscle per week with each session under about 70 minutes. The split itself matters less than weekly volume, effort and progression. If you're new to lifting or often miss days, a 3- or 4-day plan will usually build the same muscle with less fatigue.

### Is PPLUL better than PPL?

PPLUL is a better fit for five days a week. A standard push/pull/legs routine works best on three or six days, and on five days it has to rotate, so the weekday keeps changing. PPLUL keeps a fixed Monday to Friday week, trains every muscle twice and mixes moderate-rep PPL days with heavier upper and lower days. If you can train six days, regular PPL run twice through works just as well.

### Is the bro split bad for natural lifters?

No, though it's rarely the best choice. Studies that match weekly sets find only small differences between training a muscle once or twice a week. The practical problems are bigger: long sessions where the last sets are poor, two-week gaps when you miss a day, and leg days people skip. Run it with 10-15 hard sets per muscle and don't miss sessions, and it works.

### What is the Arnold split?

The Arnold split is a routine popularised by Arnold Schwarzenegger that pairs chest with back, shoulders with arms, and trains legs alone. The full version runs each workout twice a week over six days with one rest day. On five days you rotate the three workouts, so each muscle gets about 1.7 sessions a week. It suits experienced lifters with good recovery, not beginners.

### Is 5 days a week too much for beginners?

Usually, yes. Beginners progress quickly on two or three full-body sessions a week, and five days mostly adds fatigue and time without adding much muscle. The American College of Sports Medicine suggests 2-3 days a week for novices. Spend your first 6-12 months on a 3-day plan, then move to four days, and only add a fifth when your recovery and schedule clearly allow it.
