The best 4 day split workout for most people is **upper/lower**: two upper-body days and two lower-body days, usually Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday. Every muscle gets trained twice a week, and you get three full rest days. If upper/lower doesn't suit you, there are three other solid options: push/pull/legs plus an upper day, a four-day body-part split, and full body four times a week.

This guide gives you all four as complete plans: weekly schedules, every session with sets, reps and rest, the number of hard sets each muscle gets per week, and a progression rule. If you're still deciding how many days to train at all, start with our [push/pull/legs routine](/blog/push-pull-legs-routine) guide, which covers three, five and six-day schedules too.

## The four 4-day splits at a glance

| Split | Weekly layout | Each muscle trained | Session length | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Upper/lower** | Upper, Lower, rest, Upper, Lower | Twice a week | 60 to 75 min | Most lifters, beginner to intermediate |
| **PPL + upper** | Push, Pull, Legs, rest, Upper | Upper body twice, legs once | 55 to 70 min | People who want more chest, back and arms |
| **Body-part split** | Chest/tris, Back/bis, rest, Legs, Shoulders/arms | Mostly once, arms twice | 60 to 75 min | Experienced lifters who like long, focused sessions |
| **Full body x4** | A, B, rest, A, B | Four times a week (2 to 3 sets each time) | 50 to 60 min | Strength focus, people who like frequent practice |

All four can build muscle. The differences are in how the weekly work is spread out, what each session feels like, and which muscles get the most attention. A 4 day lifting split also leaves three rest days, so a missed session can usually move to one of them instead of being lost.

## Split 1: the 4 day upper/lower split

The upper lower split is the default for good reason. It hits every muscle twice a week, which Schoenfeld and colleagues' 2016 meta-analysis found beat once a week for growth when total sets were matched ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27102172/)). It also fits a normal working week.

### Upper/lower schedule

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upper A | Lower A | Rest | Upper B | Lower B | Rest | Rest |

Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday works too, as does any layout where you never train more than two days in a row. The A days are heavier (lower reps on the main lifts). The B days use more moderate weights and more isolation work.

|---|---|---|---|

| Lat pulldown | 3 x 8-12 | Cable fly | 2 x 12-15 |
| Lateral raise | 2 x 12-20 | Lateral raise | 3 x 12-20 |
| EZ-bar curl | 2 x 8-12 | Face pull | 2 x 12-20 |
| Triceps pushdown | 2 x 10-15 | Incline dumbbell curl | 2 x 10-15 |
| | | Overhead triceps extension | 2 x 10-15 |

### Lower A and Lower B

| Lower A | Sets x reps | Lower B | Sets x reps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Back squat | 4 x 5-8 | Deadlift or trap-bar deadlift | 3 x 4-6 |
| Romanian deadlift | 3 x 6-10 | Bulgarian split squat | 3 x 8-12 each leg |
| Leg press | 3 x 10-15 | Leg extension | 3 x 12-15 |
| Lying leg curl | 3 x 10-15 | Seated leg curl | 3 x 10-15 |
| Standing calf raise | 4 x 8-12 | Seated calf raise | 4 x 10-15 |
| Hanging knee raise | 3 x 10-15 | Cable crunch | 3 x 10-15 |

Rest two to three minutes on the first two lifts of each day and 60 to 90 seconds on everything else. Stop each working set with one to three good reps left. Our guide to [rest between sets](/blog/rest-between-sets-compound-vs-isolation) explains why the big lifts need longer.

### Who the upper/lower split suits

Nearly everyone who can train four days a week. Beginners get frequent practice on the main lifts, and intermediates get enough weekly sets to keep growing. The downside is that upper days run long: chest, back, shoulders and both arms in one session add up to about 20 sets. If you're short on time, do the curls and pushdowns back to back with a minute's rest between pairs.

## Split 2: push/pull/legs + upper

This hybrid takes the three [PPL workouts](/blog/push-pull-legs-routine) and adds an upper-body day at the end of the week. Chest, back, shoulders and arms get trained twice; legs get one big session. It suits lifters who want more upper-body work, or who play a sport or run, so their legs already get plenty.

### PPL + upper schedule

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Push | Pull | Legs | Rest | Upper | Rest | Rest |

Keep the upper day at least one rest day away from push and pull. Friday works because chest last trained on Monday and back on Tuesday (our guide to [muscle recovery time](/blog/how-long-does-it-take-for-muscles-to-recover) explains why that gap matters).

### PPL + upper sessions

| Push (Mon) | Sets x reps | Pull (Tue) | Sets x reps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Barbell bench press | 4 x 5-8 | Pull-up or lat pulldown | 4 x 6-10 |
| Seated dumbbell shoulder press | 3 x 8-12 | Barbell row | 3 x 6-10 |
| Incline dumbbell press | 3 x 8-12 | Seated cable row | 3 x 10-12 |
| Lateral raise | 3 x 12-20 | Face pull | 3 x 12-20 |
| Triceps pushdown | 3 x 10-15 | Dumbbell curl | 3 x 10-15 |

| Legs (Wed) | Sets x reps | Upper (Fri) | Sets x reps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Back squat | 4 x 5-8 | Incline machine or barbell press | 3 x 8-12 |
| Romanian deadlift | 3 x 6-10 | Chest-supported row | 3 x 8-12 |
| Leg press | 3 x 10-15 | Lat pulldown | 2 x 10-12 |
| Lying leg curl | 3 x 10-15 | Cable fly | 2 x 12-15 |
| Leg extension | 2 x 12-15 | Lateral raise | 3 x 12-20 |
| Standing calf raise | 4 x 8-12 | EZ-bar curl | 2 x 10-12 |
| | | Overhead triceps extension | 2 x 10-15 |

The catch is that legs get one session a week. If you want your legs to grow as much as your upper body, flip it around and run push, pull, legs and a **lower** day instead (a second leg session with hack squats, leg curls and calves).

## Split 3: the 4-day body-part split

This is the classic bodybuilding layout, trimmed to four days: chest and triceps, back and biceps, legs, then shoulders and arms. Most muscles get one long session a week; arms get two.

### Body-part schedule

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chest & triceps | Back & biceps | Rest | Legs | Shoulders & arms | Rest | Rest |

| Cable fly | 3 x 12-15 | Straight-arm pulldown | 2 x 12-15 |

### Who the body-part split suits

Experienced lifters who enjoy chasing a pump on one or two muscles a session, and anyone who likes training this way. That's a fair reason on its own, because you're more likely to keep turning up for a split you enjoy.

The research on body-part splits is mixed. A 2015 study by Schoenfeld and colleagues compared a body-part split with full-body training three times a week in trained men. The full-body group gained more on one of three muscle measures, and strength gains were similar ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25932981/)). A 2025 meta-regression by Pelland and colleagues found that weekly sets explained muscle growth far better than training frequency did ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/)). So a body-part split can work if the weekly sets are there. The practical catch is that sets nine to thirteen for one muscle in a single session are done tired, so not every set pulls its weight.

## Split 4: full body four times a week

Two full-body workouts, A and B, alternated across four days. Each session trains everything with only two or three sets per muscle, so the weekly total builds up from frequent small doses.

### Full-body schedule

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full body A | Full body B | Rest | Full body A | Full body B | Rest | Rest |

### Full body A and B

| Full body A | Sets x reps | Full body B | Sets x reps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Back squat | 3 x 5-8 | Romanian deadlift | 3 x 6-10 |
| Barbell bench press | 3 x 5-8 | Standing overhead press | 3 x 6-10 |
| Lat pulldown | 3 x 8-12 | Chest-supported row | 3 x 8-12 |
| Lying leg curl | 2 x 10-15 | Leg press | 2 x 10-15 |
| Lateral raise | 2 x 12-20 | Incline dumbbell press | 2 x 8-12 |
| Dumbbell curl | 2 x 10-15 | Triceps pushdown | 2 x 10-15 |
| Standing calf raise | 2 x 10-15 | | |

### Who full body x4 suits

Lifters whose main goal is strength on the big lifts, since practising a lift more often helps you get better at it. The Pelland meta-regression found strength gains rose with training frequency, with diminishing returns. It also suits beginners who like being in the gym often, and anyone who misses sessions now and then: miss one and every muscle still gets trained at least once that week.

The downside is that you squat or hinge four times a week, and some people's knees and lower backs dislike that. Keep the hinge and squat sets moderate and leave a rep or two in reserve.

## Weekly sets per muscle: how the four splits compare

Counting weekly hard sets per muscle is the quickest way to see what a split prioritises. The numbers below are **direct sets** from the plans above. Compound lifts also work other muscles (bench press trains triceps, rows train biceps), so arms and shoulders get more work than the numbers show.

| Muscle | Upper/lower | PPL + upper | Body-part | Full body x4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chest | 9 | 12 | 13 | 10 |
| Back | 13 | 15 | 12 | 12 |
| Side delts | 5 | 6 | 4 | 4 |
| Biceps | 4 | 5 | 9 | 4 |
| Triceps | 4 | 5 | 9 | 4 |
| Quads | 13 | 9 | 10 | 10 |
| Hamstrings | 9 | 6 | 6 | 10 |
| Calves | 8 | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| **Times each muscle is trained** | 2x | 2x upper, 1x legs | 1x (arms 2x) | 4x (delts, arms, calves 2x) |

A few things jump out:

- **Upper/lower is the most balanced.** It's a little light on chest and arms for an intermediate; if those lag, add 2 sets of cable fly to Upper A and 1 more set each of curls and pushdowns to both upper days.
- **PPL + upper favours the upper body**, by design. Hamstrings and calves get the least.
- **The body-part split piles sets into single sessions.** Thirteen chest sets on Monday is a lot to do well.
- **Full body x4 is the most even spread**, with small doses every session.

A 2017 meta-analysis by Schoenfeld, Ogborn and Krieger found that more weekly sets meant more muscle growth, in a graded way ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27433992/)). That doesn't mean more is always better: the extra sets have to be ones you recover from. Our guide on [how many sets per muscle group per week](/blog/how-many-sets-per-muscle-group-per-week) walks through setting your own targets.

## Which 4 day split workout should you pick?

Use the first line that fits you:

1. **You're not sure:** upper/lower. It's the safest 4 day split workout to run for your first year on four days.
2. **You want a bigger chest, back and arms more than bigger legs:** PPL + upper.
3. **You care most about strength on squat, bench and deadlift:** full body x4, or upper/lower with heavier A days.
4. **You love long sessions on one or two muscles and have trained for years:** body-part split.
5. **You often lose a training day mid-week:** full body x4, because every session trains everything.

If four days keeps turning into three, there's no shame in dropping to a [3-day workout split](/blog/3-day-workout-split). If you're recovering well and want a fifth day, our [5-day workout split](/blog/5-day-workout-split) guide shows how to add one.

## How to progress on a 4 day split

A split only works if the weight on the bar keeps creeping up. The simplest rule is [double progression](/blog/double-progression): add reps at the same weight until every set hits the top of the range, then add the smallest weight jump you have.

Here's the Upper A bench press, 4 x 5-8:

| Week | Weight | Reps per set | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 60 kg (135 lb) | 8, 7, 6, 6 | Same weight |
| 2 | 60 kg (135 lb) | 8, 8, 7, 6 | Same weight |
| 3 | 60 kg (135 lb) | 8, 8, 8, 7 | Same weight |
| 4 | 60 kg (135 lb) | 8, 8, 8, 8 | **Add 2.5 kg (5 lb)** |
| 5 | 62.5 kg (140 lb) | 7, 6, 6, 5 | Same weight |

And the Lower A squat, 4 x 5-8, starting at 80 kg (175 lb): once you hit 4 x 8, move to 82.5 kg (180 lb) and climb back up. Lower-body lifts usually handle 2.5 to 5 kg (5 to 10 lb) jumps for longer than upper-body lifts do.

For dumbbells, the jump is usually 2 kg or 2.5 kg per hand in a kilogram gym, or 5 lb per hand in a pound gym. That's a big jump on light exercises, which is why lateral raises and curls use wide ranges like 12 to 20.

**Each session progresses on its own.** On upper/lower, Upper A's bench press only has to beat last week's Upper A bench press, not Upper B's incline press.

### Logging your sessions

You need last week's weights and reps for every set in front of you. A notebook does the job. ## Putting it into practice

1. **Pick the split** using the list above. When in doubt, choose upper/lower.
2. **Fix your days.** Write the four gym days into your calendar and never train more than two in a row on upper/lower.
3. **Copy the sessions** and swap exercises only where equipment or joints demand it, keeping the same movement (a machine press for a barbell press, a leg press for a hack squat).
4. **Start light.** Choose weights that land you in the middle of each rep range.
5. **Log every working set**, in a notebook or in GymLedger, and apply double progression each week.
6. **Review after eight weeks.** If most lifts went up, keep going. If one muscle lags, add 2 to 4 weekly sets for it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a 4 day split good for beginners?

Yes. A 4 day upper/lower split is one of the best setups for beginners who can reliably train four days a week, because each muscle gets trained twice and the main lifts get frequent practice. Keep the sets at the lower end of the plan above for the first month, focus on clean technique, and add weight slowly. If four days is a stretch, three full-body days work just as well.

### Is upper/lower better than push/pull/legs?

Neither is better when weekly sets are similar. The real difference is scheduling. Upper/lower fits four days a week perfectly and hits everything twice. Push/pull/legs fits three or six days neatly, but on four days it either trains legs once or needs a rolling rotation. If you have exactly four days, upper/lower is usually the simpler choice.

### Which days should I train on a 4 day split?

Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday is the most common layout because it never stacks more than two days in a row and leaves the weekend free. Monday, Wednesday, Friday and Saturday works for the body-part split. On full body x4, try to keep at least one rest day between your two hardest leg sessions.

### How long should a 4 day split workout take?

Plan on 50 to 75 minutes per session, including warm-ups. Upper days on an upper/lower split run longest because they cover so many muscles. If you're regularly past 90 minutes, cut a set from each exercise or pair your arm exercises back to back, rather than shortening rest on the heavy compound lifts.

### Can you build muscle on a 4 day split?

Yes. Four days a week gives plenty of room for 10 to 15 hard sets per muscle each week, which is enough for most lifters to keep growing for years. What decides your results is whether you add weight or reps over time, eat enough protein and calories, and turn up for all four sessions most weeks.
