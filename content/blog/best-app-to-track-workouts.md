## What actually matters in a workout tracking app

Most "best gym apps" lists reward the longest feature list. In the gym you'll use about six features, hundreds of times each, usually with chalk on your hands and your heart rate somewhere north of 140. Those six decide whether you're still logging in week three.

### 1. Logging speed

A set should take two or three taps: confirm the weight, confirm the reps, done. Good apps pre-fill today's set from last time, so repeating 60 kg (135 lb) for 8 is a single tick. Bad ones make you open a keyboard for every number.

Test it on a normal session of 15 to 20 sets. If you spend more time in the app than you spend resting, the app has turned into a hobby, and you'll quit it.

### 2. Last session's numbers on screen

Seeing last session's numbers is what makes logging pay off. You walk up to the bench, the app shows 70 kg x 8, 8, 7 from last Tuesday (155 lb x 8, 8, 7), and today's job is obvious: get that last set to 8. Without it you guess, and guessing usually means lifting the same weights for months. Our guide on [how to track progressive overload](/blog/how-to-track-progressive-overload) shows how to read those numbers week to week.

### 3. A progress chart for every exercise

A per-exercise chart turns a pile of sessions into a trend line. Check two things: can you open it from inside a workout, and is it free? Boostcamp, for example, keeps per-exercise charts in its Pro plan.

### 4. A rest timer you can see from the Lock Screen

The timer should start when you tick a set, buzz when time is up, and show on the Lock Screen so you're not opening your phone after every set. Boostcamp, JEFIT, Hevy and GymLedger all mention Lock Screen or Live Activity timers on their official pages. If you're unsure how long to rest in the first place, see [how long to rest between sets](/blog/rest-between-sets-compound-vs-isolation).

### 5. Templates that fit your split

You should be able to build your own routine for any split and start it in one tap. Watch the free-tier caps here, because routine limits are a common paywall. Strong's free version allows three custom routines, which covers push/pull/legs exactly and nothing more. If you're still designing your week, start with [how to build your own workout split](/blog/how-to-build-your-own-workout-split).

### 6. Platform

Do you need Android, an Apple Watch app, or a web version for planning at a desk? This one rules apps out fast, so check it before you fall in love with a feature list.

### 7. How you pay

There are three models: free with limits, a subscription, or a one-time payment. Also find out what happens to your history if you stop paying. Hevy's App Store listing, for instance, says Pro includes your complete training history, which tells you the free plan shows less of it.

### 8. Privacy and your data

Social apps have profiles, feeds and leaderboards. Some lifters love that, others don't want their body weight anywhere near a feed. Also check whether you can export your data and delete your account, and where any AI features send what you type.

| What to check | Quick test in week one | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| Logging speed | Time how long a set takes to log | You need the keyboard for every set |
| Last session | Open an exercise mid-workout | You have to leave the workout to find history |
| Charts | Look at one exercise after 3 sessions | Charts are locked or hard to find |
| Rest timer | Lock your phone during rest | You have to open the phone to see the countdown |
| Templates | Build your real split | You hit a routine cap before you finish |
| Price | Read the in-app purchase list | Features you rely on are locked after a trial |

## The apps, one by one

### Strong: the best pure logger

[Strong](https://www.strong.app/) pitches itself as the simplest workout tracker: a fast log that stays out of your way. Its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/strong-workout-tracker-gym-log/id464254577) says it's built for barbell programs like Starting Strength and StrongLifts 5x5 but adapts to any routine. The Apple Watch app is fully featured and can log a workout with or without your iPhone, and the official site lists Android too.

The listing covers the logging basics well: an auto countdown rest timer, warm-up, failure and drop set tags, supersets, volume and 1RM graphs, a warm-up calculator, a plate calculator, notes and progress pictures, and kg, lb or a mix of both. You can export everything as CSV, and the site lists RPE.

**Pricing:** the free version saves unlimited workouts but limits you to 3 custom routines. Strong PRO is $4.99 a month or $29.99 a year in the US, and the in-app purchase list also includes a one-time "Forever" option.

**Good for:** lifters who write their own program and want the log to do one job quickly.

**Trade-offs:** three routines is enough for a basic push/pull/legs and not much else. It won't plan sessions for you, and it has no nutrition side.

### Hevy: the best free app with a community

[Hevy](https://www.hevyapp.com/) is free with no ads, and its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hevy-workout-tracker-gym-log/id1458862350) says more than 15 million lifters use it. The listing covers the logging basics: weights, reps, duration and RPE, last session's weights and reps shown next to each exercise, supersets, drop sets, warm-ups and failure sets, automatic rest timers, and graphs and PRs for every exercise. The Apple Watch app logs sets and starts rest timers without you touching your phone. The official site adds Android, Wear OS and a web version.

The social side is a big part of the product. You can follow friends and athletes, see their workouts in a feed, compare lifts, and recent updates added gym leaderboards. If a friend's comment gets you to the gym on a wet Tuesday, that's worth having.

**Pricing:** free. Hevy Pro adds Hevy Trainer (a program built around your experience, goal and equipment), unlimited routines and custom exercises, and your complete training history. The US App Store lists Pro at $2.99 a month, $23.99 a year, or $74.99 one-time.

**Good for:** lifters who want a polished free tracker and don't mind, or actively like, a social layer.

**Trade-offs:** the free plan caps routines and custom exercises. And if a feed is exactly what you're trying to avoid, we compared [Hevy alternatives without a social feed](/blog/hevy-alternatives-without-social-feed).

### StrengthLog: the most generous free tier

[StrengthLog](https://www.strengthlog.com/) comes from a team that also publishes a big library of training articles and programs, and its free plan is the most complete on this list. According to its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/strengthlog-workout-tracker/id1434229662), free users get unlimited workout logging with no ads, kg and lbs, 600+ exercises, unlimited custom exercises, routines with linear progression, PRs and history, volume statistics, a rest timer, a plate calculator and 1RM estimates. The site lists apps for iOS and Android, plus Apple Watch and Wear OS.

There's a social side, but a light one. You can connect with friends, compare PRs and run challenges, and friends see summaries of your workouts. The listing says your full training log stays private.

**Pricing:** free forever for the features above. The website says the free plan allows up to five programs and templates combined. Premium is a subscription for 1, 3 or 12 months, and first-time users can try it free for 14 days. Premium adds double and triple progression, unlimited programs, advanced statistics, muscle heat maps, and RPE and reps-in-reserve logging.

**Good for:** anyone who wants a serious tracker without paying.

**Trade-offs:** double progression is a Premium feature. If you follow the [double progression method](/blog/double-progression), you'll either pay or apply the rule yourself by reading your log.

### Boostcamp: best for running a proven program

[Boostcamp](https://www.boostcamp.app/) is built around programs. Its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/boostcamp-workout-tracker/id1529354455) describes 11,000+ programs, including 130+ written by coaches, with names lifters will recognize: nSuns, GZCLP, 5/3/1, Reddit PPL, PHUL and PHAT. You log in the same app, with RPE, RIR and estimated 1RM, a rest timer, a plate calculator, supersets and drop sets. Live Activities put your current set and rest timer on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. There's also an AI coach that builds a multi-week program from a short questionnaire.

**Pricing:** the official site says tracking, the program library, the program builder and the rest timer are free. Boostcamp Pro is $14.99 a month or $59.99 a year with a 7-day free trial, and adds coach-exclusive programs, personalized programs, auto progression, per-exercise records and charts, and its Strength Score.

**Good for:** beginners and intermediates who want to follow a known program instead of writing one.

**Trade-offs:** per-exercise charts are a Pro feature, and seeing that trend is a big reason to log at all. The App Store lists iPhone and iPad but no watch app. If you write your own simple routine, a program library this big is more app than you need.

### Liftosaur: best for automating your progression

[Liftosaur](https://www.liftosaur.com/) is the tinkerer's pick. You write programs in Liftoscript, a short text format, and the app raises or lowers your weights and reps after each session based on the rules you set. A rule like "add 2.5 kg (5 lb) when I hit all my reps" is one line. Its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/liftosaur-scriptable-workouts/id1661880849) includes built-in programs such as GZCLP and other GZCL variants, 5/3/1, the r/Fitness Basic Beginner Routine and Strong Curves, plus rest timers, a plate calculator, graphs and body measurements.

It runs on iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch per the App Store, and the official site adds Android and a web app with a desktop program editor.

**Pricing:** free to download. The US App Store lists a $4.99 monthly subscription, $39.99 yearly, or $99.99 one-time. The site doesn't spell out exactly which features are free, so check in the app.

**Good for:** lifters who already know their progression scheme and want the math done for them.

**Trade-offs:** there's a learning curve. If you just want to type today's sets and go, it's a lot of machinery.

### Fitbod: best if you want the app to plan each session

[Fitbod](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fitbod-workout-gym-planner/id1041517543) is a planner first and a log second. Its App Store listing describes AI-generated workouts built around your goals, fitness level and equipment, which adapt as you edit them and vary exercises over time. It also mentions recovery tracking, 1,000+ exercises with video, and Apple Health, Strava and Fitbit integration. The App Store lists iPhone and Apple Watch, and its [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fitbod.fitbod) covers Android and Wear OS.

**Pricing:** a free trial, then a monthly or yearly subscription. The US App Store lists monthly plans at $12.99 to $15.99 and yearly plans at $79.99 to $95.99.

**Good for:** people who'd rather be told what to do today than write a program.

**Trade-offs:** if you already run a program you like, the AI is in the way. There's no free tier after the trial, and it's one of the pricier options here.

### JEFIT: best big exercise library with AI plans

[JEFIT](https://www.jefit.com/) is built around a huge exercise library: its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/jefit-workout-plan-gym-tracker/id449810000) mentions 1,400+ exercises with HD video. It covers custom workouts and exercises, supersets, a custom rest timer, warm-up, drop and failure set tags, notes, built-in programs, charts for volume and weekly sets per muscle, a muscle map, and a muscle recovery chart. The rest timer shows on your Lock Screen, and the Apple Watch app live-syncs with your phone. The official site lists iOS, Android, Apple Watch, Wear OS and web.

**Pricing:** the site says logging, the rest timer, the exercise library, history and PRs are free, with three watch sessions per account. JEFIT Elite adds AI-built Adaptive Plans, suggested weights and reps for each session, unlimited watch sessions and deeper analysis including recovery. The US App Store lists Elite at $12.99 a month or $69.99 a year.

**Good for:** lifters who want variety, video demos and an AI plan in the same place.

**Trade-offs:** the most interesting features sit in Elite, and it's a busier app than a pure logger.

### GymLedger: training, recovery and protein in one iPhone app

[GymLedger](/) is our app, so here's the plain version. It's a workout tracker for iPhone that also covers the two things lifters usually open other apps for: recovery and protein.

**Training:** you build custom templates with your exercises, sets and rep ranges for any split, and add custom exercises. The dashboard puts today's workout at the top (the next one in your rotation, or tap its name to pick another), and you start it in one tap. Set, rep and weight logging is fast, in kg or lbs. The rest timer has haptic feedback and keeps counting on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. You get a strength chart for every exercise and a body-weight chart.

**Recovery:** the Recovery tab shows a body map that colours each muscle as fresh, recovering, or still sore or fatigued after your workouts, and suggests the workout that fits best today.

**Protein:** in the Diet tab you type what you ate in plain words, like "2 eggs and toast", and AI estimates calories, protein, carbs and fat. Your daily protein target is set from your body weight at 1.6 g per kg, so an 80 kg (176 lb) lifter gets a target of 128 g. The dashboard shows today's protein against that target and how your week is going.

**Everything else:** it syncs across your iPhones, supports Sign in with Apple, Google or email, and comes in 12 languages, including Hindi. Training data isn't sold or used for advertising. Meal descriptions are sent to OpenAI only to estimate nutrition, and you can delete your account and data from inside the app.

**Pricing:** a subscription is required. In the US it's $2.99 a month with a 3-day free trial for new subscribers, or $29.99 a year with a 7-day free trial.

**Trade-offs, stated plainly:**

- **iPhone only.** There's no Android version and no watch app.
- **No free tier.** After the trial you pay, so if free-forever matters, StrengthLog or Hevy is the better fit.
- **No social feed** and no AI-generated programs. You write your own templates.
- **Fewer advanced logging fields.** It doesn't have RPE, supersets, a plate calculator or data export. Strong, StrengthLog Premium and Boostcamp cover more of that ground.

**Good for:** iPhone lifters who run their own split and want their training log, recovery and protein in one place.

Start with **Boostcamp** and pick a beginner program from its library, or let **Fitbod** build sessions for you if you don't want to think about it at all. If you'd rather write a simple full-body routine yourself, **StrengthLog** or **Hevy** costs nothing.

## One app or three: logging, recovery and protein

A lot of lifters end up with a stack: a workout log, a calorie counter, and maybe something that tells them whether their legs are still cooked from Monday. That works, but every extra app is another thing to open, another subscription, and another place your data lives.

Few loggers track recovery. If you train the same muscle again before it has recovered, the session suffers and the log shows a stall that isn't really a strength problem. Our guide to [how long it takes muscles to recover](/blog/how-long-does-it-take-for-muscles-to-recover) covers the typical windows. Fitbod and JEFIT both mention recovery tracking. GymLedger shows it as a body map and suggests the workout that fits best today, so you see that your chest is still recovering before you start another push day.

Protein is the other piece. Many lifters set a daily protein target, and it's easier to hit when you see it next to your training. Typing "chicken rice bowl and a protein shake" into GymLedger's Diet tab takes a few seconds, against searching a food database item by item. If you're weighing up text-based logging against photo-based apps, see our comparison of [AI calorie counters](/blog/ai-calorie-counter), and for the target itself, [how much protein you need to build muscle](/blog/how-much-protein-to-build-muscle).

## Switching apps without losing your progress

Moving apps feels risky because your history is in the old one. It's less of a problem than it looks.

1. **Write down your current working weights.** Before you switch, note your last top sets for the main lifts. "Bench 70 kg x 8 (155 lb x 8), squat 100 kg x 5 (220 lb x 5), row 60 kg x 10 (135 lb x 10)" is all you need to start in the new app.
2. **Check export and import.** Strong lists CSV export. Other apps vary, and GymLedger doesn't import or export data, so plan on starting fresh with your working weights.
3. **Keep the old app for a month.** Leave it installed for reference until the new app has a few weeks of your history.
4. **Keep your exercise names the same.** "Incline DB press" in one app and "Incline dumbbell bench" in another will split one exercise's chart into two. If you log dumbbells, pick per hand or total and stick to it; our guide on [logging dumbbells per hand or total](/blog/log-dumbbells-per-hand-or-total) explains why.

## Putting it into practice

Pick one app and commit to it. Install it, build your real split as templates, and log every working set for two weeks. Two weeks is enough to find out whether you'll keep using it, which matters more than any feature list.

At the end of the two weeks, ask three questions. Did logging ever slow your session down? Could you see last session's numbers before every set? Did you hit a paywall on something you rely on? If the answers are no, yes and no, you've found the best app to track workouts for you, whatever any ranking says.

If you're on iPhone and want your log, recovery and protein in one place, try GymLedger during its free trial. If you need Android, a watch app or a free-forever plan, one of the other apps above will serve you better, and that's fine too.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best free app to track workouts?

As of November 2026, StrengthLog has the most generous free plan: unlimited logging, unlimited custom exercises, a rest timer, a plate calculator and 1RM estimates, with up to five programs and templates. Hevy is free with no ads and adds a social feed, but caps routines and custom exercises. Strong's free version saves unlimited workouts with three custom routines.

### Is Strong or Hevy better for lifting?

Both log sets, reps and weight well and run on iPhone, Apple Watch and Android. Strong is a quieter, no-frills logger with a free version limited to three routines. Hevy is free with no ads, shows last session's numbers next to each exercise, and puts a social feed at the centre. Pick Strong for focus and Hevy for community.

### What is the best gym app for beginners?

If you want someone else to write the plan, Boostcamp lets you follow a proven beginner program for free, and Fitbod builds each session for you on a subscription that starts with a free trial. If you already have a simple routine, a fast logger like Strong, StrengthLog or Hevy is enough. What matters most is that you log every session and try to beat last time.

### Do I need an app to track workouts, or is a notebook enough?

A notebook works. The basics are the same anywhere: exercise, weight, sets and reps for every working set. An app to track workouts saves time by showing last session's numbers automatically, running a rest timer and drawing charts. If you prefer paper or a spreadsheet, use a consistent template so you can compare sessions at a glance.

### Which workout tracking apps work on Android and Apple Watch?

According to their official pages and listings as of November 2026, Strong, Hevy, StrengthLog, Liftosaur, Fitbod and JEFIT offer both Android and Apple Watch apps. Boostcamp lists Android and iPhone but no watch app. GymLedger runs on iPhone only, with no watch app, so it suits lifters who log from their phone.
