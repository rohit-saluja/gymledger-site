A PPL routine splits your training into three workouts: **push** (chest, shoulders, triceps), **pull** (back, biceps, rear delts) and **legs** (quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves). Run it three days a week and each muscle gets one hard session; run it six days a week and each muscle gets two. If neither fits your week, you can roll it over four or five days. Below you'll find beginner and intermediate versions as full workouts with sets, reps and rest, plus where to put rest days, how many sets each muscle gets, and one rule for adding weight.

In short, beginners start with the three-day plan, lifters with a year or more of steady training who can get to the gym six times a week move to the six-day plan, and everyone adds weight with [double progression](/blog/double-progression).

## What is a PPL routine?

A push pull legs routine groups muscles by the movement they do, not by body part. Pressing muscles train together, pulling muscles train together, and the lower body gets its own day.

| Day | Muscles trained | Typical movements |
|---|---|---|
| **Push** | Chest, front and side delts, triceps | Bench press, overhead press, dips, lateral raises, pushdowns |
| **Pull** | Lats, upper back, rear delts, biceps, forearms | Pull-ups, rows, pulldowns, face pulls, curls |
| **Legs** | Quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves (and usually abs) | Squats, Romanian deadlifts, leg press, leg curls, calf raises |

Your triceps already work hard on every bench press, so PPL trains them on the same day and then rests them while you pull and squat. Your biceps help on every row, so they finish off pull day. Muscles that work together also rest together. Compare that with chest on Monday and triceps on Tuesday, where your triceps get hammered two days running and never get a proper day off.

### Why PPL is so popular

The same three workouts run three, four, five or six days a week, so the plan survives a change of schedule. There are only three sessions to remember and they always come in the same order. Each session also stays focused: you warm up your shoulders properly once, on push day, and leave them alone on leg day.

It isn't magic, though. A 2016 meta-analysis by Schoenfeld and colleagues found that training a muscle **twice a week** beat once a week for growth when total sets were matched ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27102172/)). A larger 2025 meta-regression by Pelland and colleagues found that weekly sets drove growth far more clearly than frequency did ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/)). So how often you hit a muscle matters less than how many hard sets it gets across the week. PPL is a convenient way to fit those sets in. It isn't the only way, and our guide to [building your own workout split](/blog/how-to-build-your-own-workout-split) covers the alternatives.

## PPL schedules: 3, 4, 5 or 6 days a week

Pick the schedule you will turn up for. A six-day plan you run four days a week is worse than a four-day plan you never miss.

| Schedule | Days per week | Each muscle trained | Session length | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3-day PPL | 3 | Once a week | 60 to 75 min | Beginners, busy weeks |
| Rolling PPL, 4 days | 4 | About 1.3 times a week | 60 to 70 min | Intermediates with four free days |
| Rolling PPL, 5 days | 5 | About 1.7 times a week | 55 to 70 min | Intermediates, most weeks |
| 6-day PPL | 6 | Twice a week | 50 to 65 min | Trained lifters who recover well |

### 3-day PPL

Each workout runs once a week, with a rest day between sessions.

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Push | Rest | Pull | Rest | Legs | Rest | Rest |

Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday works just as well. The trade-off is that each muscle gets trained only once a week, so every session has to carry a full week's work for its muscles. That's fine early on. If you have exactly three days and want each muscle twice, a full-body plan does that better; our [3-day workout split](/blog/3-day-workout-split) guide compares the two.

### 6-day PPL

Every workout runs twice a week. There are two good places for the rest day:

| Option | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A: rest at the end** | Push | Pull | Legs | Push | Pull | Legs | Rest |
| **B: rest in the middle** | Push | Pull | Legs | Rest | Push | Pull | Legs |

Option A is the classic. Option B splits the six sessions into two blocks of three, so your knees and lower back get a break mid-week; the cost is training on Sunday. Either way, six sessions a week is a commitment. Run it only if you're sleeping well, eating enough and still hitting your reps in the sixth session of the week.

### Rolling PPL over 4 or 5 days

A rolling (or rotating) schedule ignores the calendar. You train on your gym days and always do the **next workout in the order**, whatever day it is. That makes PPL work on any number of days.

**Four days a week (Mon, Tue, Thu, Fri):**

| Week | Mon | Tue | Thu | Fri |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Push | Pull | Legs | Push |
| 2 | Pull | Legs | Push | Pull |
| 3 | Legs | Push | Pull | Legs |

**Five days a week (Mon, Tue, Wed, Fri, Sat):**

| Week | Mon | Tue | Wed | Fri | Sat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Push | Pull | Legs | Push | Pull |
| 2 | Legs | Push | Pull | Legs | Push |
| 3 | Pull | Legs | Push | Pull | Legs |

Every session comes round four times in three weeks on the four-day plan and five times on the five-day plan. If you'd rather have a fixed four-day week, upper/lower is usually the better fit; see our [4-day workout split](/blog/4-day-workout-split) guide. If five days is your number, our [5-day workout split](/blog/5-day-workout-split) post covers the PPL plus upper/lower hybrid (PPLUL) that a lot of lifters prefer.

### Where to put rest days

1. **Never more than three hard days in a row** unless you are running the full six-day plan and recovering well.
2. **Rest after legs if you can.** Leg day is the most tiring session for most people.
3. **Keep the order.** A rest day pauses the rotation; it doesn't reset it.
4. **Watch the lower back.** Barbell rows on pull day and squats plus Romanian deadlifts on leg day both load it. If you add conventional deadlifts, don't put two hinge-heavy days back to back, and if your lower back complains, swap the barbell row for a chest-supported row.

A muscle you trained hard usually needs a couple of days before it's ready to go hard again, and how long varies with the person and the session. Our guide to [how long it takes muscles to recover](/blog/how-long-does-it-take-for-muscles-to-recover) goes through it in detail.

## Beginner push/pull/legs routine (3 days a week)

This is a beginner push/pull/legs routine built for the three-day schedule. Each workout has five exercises and about 16 working sets, which takes 60 to 75 minutes including warm-ups.

**How to read the tables:** "4 x 6-10" means four working sets of 6 to 10 reps. Warm-up sets don't count. Stop each working set with about one to three good reps left in the tank. Rest two to three minutes on the big lifts and 60 to 90 seconds on isolation work (here's [why the rest times differ](/blog/rest-between-sets-compound-vs-isolation)).

### Push day (beginner)

| Exercise | Sets x reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|
| Barbell bench press | 4 x 6-10 | 2-3 min |
| Seated dumbbell shoulder press | 3 x 8-12 | 2 min |
| Incline dumbbell press | 3 x 8-12 | 2 min |
| Lateral raise | 3 x 12-20 | 60-90 s |
| Triceps rope pushdown | 3 x 10-15 | 60-90 s |

### Pull day (beginner)

| Exercise | Sets x reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|
| Lat pulldown | 4 x 8-12 | 2 min |
| Seated cable row | 3 x 8-12 | 2 min |
| Chest-supported dumbbell row | 3 x 10-12 | 90 s |
| Face pull | 3 x 12-20 | 60-90 s |
| Dumbbell curl | 3 x 10-15 | 60-90 s |

### Leg day (beginner)

| Exercise | Sets x reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|
| Back squat (or goblet squat) | 4 x 6-10 | 2-3 min |
| Romanian deadlift | 3 x 8-10 | 2-3 min |
| Leg press | 3 x 10-15 | 2 min |
| Lying leg curl | 3 x 10-15 | 90 s |
| Standing calf raise | 3 x 10-15 | 60-90 s |

### Picking starting weights

Start lighter than your ego wants. On your first session, use a weight that lets you finish every set at the middle of the range (8 reps on a 6 to 10 exercise) with two or three reps to spare. A typical beginner might bench 40 kg (about 90 lb) for 4 x 8, squat 50 kg (about 110 lb) for 4 x 8, and use 10 kg (about 20 lb) dumbbells per hand for shoulder presses. Those numbers are examples, not targets. The weight you start with matters far less than whether it goes up from there.

If plate maths in a kilogram gym is new to you, our guide on [how to load a barbell in kg](/blog/how-to-load-a-barbell-in-kg) covers the common combinations.

## Intermediate PPL routine (6 days a week)

Once the three-day plan stops moving, or you simply want more training, switch to six days with **A and B versions** of each workout. Running the same session twice a week works, but alternating exercises spreads the stress across joints and covers each muscle from more than one angle.

This intermediate push pull legs workout runs in the order Push A, Pull A, Legs A, Push B, Pull B, Legs B.

### Push A and Push B

| Push A | Sets x reps | Push B | Sets x reps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Barbell bench press | 4 x 5-8 | Standing overhead press | 4 x 6-10 |
| Incline dumbbell press | 3 x 8-12 | Incline machine or barbell press | 3 x 8-12 |
| Seated dumbbell shoulder press | 2 x 8-12 | Cable fly | 3 x 12-15 |
| Cable lateral raise | 3 x 12-20 | Dumbbell lateral raise | 3 x 12-20 |
| Overhead triceps extension | 3 x 10-15 | Triceps pushdown | 3 x 10-15 |

### Pull A and Pull B

![A man doing pull-ups on a bar in an indoor gym](/blog/push-pull-legs-routine/pull-ups.webp)

| Pull A | Sets x reps | Pull B | Sets x reps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Barbell row | 4 x 6-10 | Pull-up (assisted or weighted) | 4 x 5-10 |
| Lat pulldown | 3 x 8-12 | Seated cable row | 3 x 8-12 |
| Chest-supported row | 2 x 10-12 | Straight-arm pulldown | 2 x 12-15 |
| Face pull | 3 x 12-20 | Reverse pec deck | 3 x 12-20 |
| EZ-bar curl | 3 x 8-12 | Incline dumbbell curl | 3 x 10-15 |

### Legs A and Legs B

| Legs A | Sets x reps | Legs B | Sets x reps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Back squat | 4 x 5-8 | Hack squat or front squat | 4 x 8-12 |
| Romanian deadlift | 3 x 6-10 | Seated or lying leg curl | 3 x 10-15 |
| Leg press | 3 x 10-15 | Bulgarian split squat | 2 x 8-12 each leg |
| Lying leg curl | 3 x 10-15 | Leg extension | 2 x 12-15 |
| Standing calf raise | 4 x 8-12 | 45-degree back extension | 2 x 10-15 |
| | | Seated calf raise | 4 x 10-15 |

Abs fit at the end of either leg day: 2 or 3 sets of hanging knee raises or cable crunches is plenty.

## Weekly sets per muscle on a PPL routine

Counting hard sets per muscle is the fastest way to check whether a program is too light or too heavy. The table counts **direct** sets only. Compound lifts also train other muscles (bench press hits triceps, rows hit biceps), so the real total for small muscles is higher than it looks.

| Muscle | Beginner 3-day | Intermediate 6-day |
|---|---|---|
| Chest | 7 | 13 |
| Back (lats and upper back) | 10 | 18 |
| Side delts | 3 | 6 |
| Rear delts | 3 | 6 |
| Biceps | 3 (+ pulling) | 6 (+ pulling) |
| Triceps | 3 (+ pressing) | 6 (+ pressing) |
| Quads | 7 | 15 |
| Hamstrings | 6 | 11 |
| Calves | 3 | 8 |

Schoenfeld, Ogborn and Krieger's 2017 meta-analysis found a graded dose-response relationship between weekly sets and muscle growth ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27433992/)), and the 2025 Pelland meta-regression found the gains keep coming with more sets but taper off. In practice, the beginner numbers above are enough for the first months, and the intermediate numbers sit in the range most hypertrophy programs use. For how to set your own targets, read our guide on [how many sets per muscle group per week](/blog/how-many-sets-per-muscle-group-per-week).

Two adjustments worth knowing:

- **A lagging muscle** can take 2 to 4 more sets a week. Add them to the day that already trains it.
- **Arms on a three-day plan** only get one direct session a week. If your arms matter to you, and they probably do, add 2 sets of curls to push day and 2 sets of pushdowns to pull day.

## Push pull legs exercises: swaps for every day

Your gym might not have a hack squat, or your shoulder might hate the barbell bench. Swap like for like: same movement, same rep range.

| Slot | First choice | Swaps |
|---|---|---|
| Push: main press | Barbell bench press | Dumbbell bench press, machine chest press, Smith machine bench |
| Push: vertical press | Overhead press | Seated dumbbell press, machine shoulder press, landmine press |
| Push: second chest | Incline dumbbell press | Incline machine press, dips, cable fly |
| Push: side delts | Lateral raise | Cable lateral raise, machine lateral raise |
| Push: triceps | Rope pushdown | Overhead cable extension, skull crusher, close-grip bench |
| Pull: vertical | Pull-up | Lat pulldown, assisted pull-up, single-arm pulldown |
| Pull: horizontal | Barbell row | Seated cable row, chest-supported row, one-arm dumbbell row |
| Pull: rear delts | Face pull | Reverse pec deck, rear-delt dumbbell fly |
| Pull: biceps | EZ-bar curl | Dumbbell curl, hammer curl, cable curl, preacher curl |
| Legs: squat | Back squat | Goblet squat, hack squat, leg press, front squat |
| Legs: hinge | Romanian deadlift | Dumbbell RDL, 45-degree back extension, good morning |
| Legs: hamstrings | Lying leg curl | Seated leg curl, Nordic curl, stability-ball curl |
| Legs: calves | Standing calf raise | Seated calf raise, calf press on the leg press |

Pick your swaps once and keep them for at least six to eight weeks. If you change exercises every session, you can't tell whether you're getting stronger or just doing something new.

## How to progress on a PPL routine

The program above only works if the numbers go up. The simplest way to make that happen is **double progression**:

1. Every exercise has a rep range, like 6 to 10.
2. Keep the weight the same and add reps until **every set** reaches the top of the range.
3. Then add the smallest weight jump you have and work back up from the bottom.

Here's how it looks on the beginner bench press, 4 x 6-10:

| Week | Weight | Reps per set | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 40 kg (90 lb) | 9, 8, 7, 7 | Same weight |
| 2 | 40 kg (90 lb) | 10, 9, 8, 8 | Same weight |
| 3 | 40 kg (90 lb) | 10, 10, 9, 9 | Same weight |
| 4 | 40 kg (90 lb) | 10, 10, 10, 10 | **Add 2.5 kg (5 lb)** |
| 5 | 42.5 kg (95 lb) | 8, 8, 7, 7 | Same weight |

That 2.5 kg jump is about 6 percent, which fits the American College of Sports Medicine's [position stand on progression](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/): it suggests raising the load by 2 to 10 percent once you can do one or two reps more than your target.

On dumbbells, the jump is usually 2 kg or 2.5 kg per hand in a kilogram gym, or 5 lb per hand in a pound gym. That's a big percentage jump on a 10 kg (20 lb) lateral raise, which is why the isolation ranges above are wide (12 to 20). Our [double progression](/blog/double-progression) guide covers the fixes for big dumbbell jumps.

On the six-day plan, each A and B session progresses on its own. Push A's bench press only has to beat last week's Push A bench press.

**When progress stalls** for three weeks on several lifts at once, check sleep and food first, then take a lighter week. Our [deload week](/blog/deload-week) guide explains how.

### Log every set

Double progression needs last session's numbers in front of you, set by set. A notebook works. If you use [GymLedger](/), you build Push, Pull and Legs (or Push A, Push B and so on) as workout templates with the rep range for each exercise. The dashboard puts the next workout in your rotation at the top, so you never have to remember whether today is Pull A or Pull B, and the strength chart for each exercise shows whether the weight is climbing.

## What to do when you miss a day

When you miss a session, **do the next workout in the order** on your next gym day and let everything shift by a day. Don't skip the missed session and don't cram two into one.

- **One missed day:** pick up where you left off. If it was legs, legs is next.
- **A few days off:** same rule. Your weights won't have gone anywhere.
- **A week or more off:** come back at about 90 percent of your previous weights for the first session or two.

A rolling schedule makes this painless because there's no calendar to fall behind. Our full guide on [missing a day on push/pull/legs](/blog/missed-a-day-push-pull-legs) covers merging sessions and coming back after longer breaks. GymLedger's Recovery tab also helps here: its body map colours each muscle by how recovered it is after your recent workouts and suggests the workout that fits best today.

## Common PPL routine mistakes

### Turning push day into chest day

Bench, incline, decline, fly, dips, cable crossover, then "a bit of shoulders". By the sixth chest exercise you're doing tired reps that add little. Two chest exercises per push day is plenty when you train push twice a week.

### Skipping legs because it's hard

It is hard. It's also a third of the program. If leg day keeps falling off the week, it's probably landing on the same awkward day every time. A rolling schedule moves it around, so it can't get stuck there.

### Doing the six-day plan on three-day recovery

Six days a week on four hours of sleep and a bowl of cereal ends with stalled lifts and achy elbows. If your numbers are dropping across the board, run the five-day rolling version for a block.

### Changing the plan every week

New exercises feel productive. Beating your own numbers on the same exercises is what builds muscle. Keep the plan for at least eight weeks before you judge it.

### Ignoring rear delts and hamstrings

They're easy to skip because you can't see them in the mirror. Rear-delt work and leg curls are in every pull and leg day above for that reason.

## Putting it into practice

1. **Choose your schedule:** three days if you're new or busy, rolling four or five days if that's what your week allows, six days if you've trained for a year or more and recover well.
2. **Copy the workouts** for your level. Change exercises only where your gym or joints demand it, using the swap table.
3. **Pick starting weights** that put you in the middle of each rep range with clean reps.
4. **Log every working set**, then add reps first and weight second.
5. **Keep the order** when you miss a day.
6. **Review after eight weeks.** If most lifts went up, keep going. If they didn't, look at sleep, food and weekly sets before you blame the split.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a PPL routine good for beginners?

Yes, as long as it's the three-day version with sensible volume. A beginner push/pull/legs routine trains each muscle once a week, which is enough to build muscle and strength in the first months. Full-body training three times a week is the other good option, since each muscle gets more frequent practice, but for a beginner the difference is small. The plan you enjoy and stick with matters more.

### Is a 3-day PPL enough to build muscle?

It can be. Three days of push, pull and legs gives each major muscle around 6 to 10 direct sets a week in the plan above, which is enough for steady progress early on. The limit shows up later, when you need more weekly sets than fit comfortably into one session per muscle. At that point, a rolling five-day or full six-day schedule spreads the work out.

### How long should a push pull legs workout take?

Most sessions take 50 to 75 minutes, including warm-ups. Five exercises and 15 to 17 working sets, with two to three minutes of rest on compound lifts and about a minute on isolation work, lands in that range. If you regularly go past 90 minutes, cut the last exercise or a set from each lift rather than rushing your rest periods.

### Is PPL better than an upper/lower split?

Neither is better for muscle growth when weekly sets are similar. PPL fits three or six days a week neatly and keeps each session focused. Upper/lower fits four days a week better and trains everything twice. Choose by the number of days you can train reliably: three or six days suits PPL, four suits upper/lower, and five works with a rolling PPL or a hybrid.

### Where do deadlifts go in a push pull legs routine?

Either day works, as long as you don't load the lower back hard two days running. Many lifters put conventional deadlifts first on pull day for 2 or 3 heavy sets, then use Romanian deadlifts on leg day. Others put them on leg day and keep pull day to rows and pull-ups. Pick one spot, keep it there, and progress it like any other lift.
